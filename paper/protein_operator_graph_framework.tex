\begin{abstract}
Protein sequence models use the same words---coupling, memory, attention, message, and
interaction---for objects with different sample spaces and probabilistic meanings.  We introduce a
typed framework that compares these objects without identifying a computational route with a
statistical or physical interaction.  Each residue position carries a centered feature fiber, and
each directed edge carries a context-measurable operator between fibers.  The complete audit object
also records support, interaction order, state dynamics, normalization, estimation, nuisance
assumptions, and validation endpoint.  In the categorical case, weighted double projection selects
the unique reference-centered bivariate representative of a Potts pair-table parameter class.  We
derive the transformation law between reference choices and show that exact marginal-preserving
matrix scaling factors through the same quotient.

Potts models and protein language models provide the principal worked example.  A projected
substitution audit exactly recovers an additive Potts coupling.  Applied to a nonlinear sequence
model, it gives a background-dependent effective operator whose mean is the best static
approximation and whose orthogonal fluctuation measures dynamic context dependence.  Under a fixed
product reference, functional ANOVA identifies this fluctuation exactly with higher-order
source-coordinate components valued in the target contrast fiber.  Reciprocity supplies the additional compatibility
condition for directed additive responses to define a joint Potts energy.  We then distinguish this
response object from attention routes, latent memories, scalar contact shadows, MSA estimators, and
biological epistasis.  Phylogeny is represented separately as tree-constrained categorical
transport through latent ancestors on the MSA sequence axis; this makes explicit why an MSA
attention route may exploit ancestry without thereby identifying a tree.  For row-stochastic
routes, a stationary-measure adjoint further separates
reversible transport from directed circulation; an undirected Laplacian is then an explicit lossy
shadow rather than the native Transformer graph.  Scalar, signed, and connection Laplacians further
separate positional smoothness from transported categorical coherence.  The result is a mathematical coordinate
system for translating among
statistical physics, probability, and neural computation while keeping identification, execution,
estimation, and validation as separate claims.  In this view, graph structure is a relational
inductive bias: it specifies how typed entities may exchange shared computations, but does not by
itself identify what the resulting edges mean scientifically.
\end{abstract}

\BookChapter{Introduction}

Protein sequence models use the word ``interaction'' for several mathematically different
objects.  Direct-coupling analysis assigns a $q\times q$ Potts table to a pair of residue
positions.  A graph neural network assigns a message map.  Transformer attention assigns a
normalized scalar route, while a protein language model can be probed by the change in its output
under a categorical substitution.  All four objects are indexed by positions $(i,j)$ and may
predict contacts, but they do not share a sample space, invariance class, or probabilistic meaning.
Pair indexing alone is therefore not a definition of interaction.

The ambiguity is visible in the shared use of softmax.  A Potts conditional normalizes over the
$q$ residue identities at one site, attention normalizes over source positions, and modern
Hopfield retrieval normalizes over stored memories.  These operations use the same numerical
kernel but define distributions on different variables.  Likewise, a Potts Hamiltonian scores a
complete sequence, a Hopfield Lyapunov function controls a retrieval state, and an attention score
usually controls only a computational route.  A useful unification must preserve these types; it
cannot declare every matrix an energy or every route a statistical coupling.

There is a second, categorical ambiguity.  A raw table $T_{ij}(a,b)$ can contain a constant, a
function of $a$ alone, a function of $b$ alone, and a genuinely bivariate term.  These pieces are
interchangeable under the additive parameter redundancy conventionally called a Potts gauge.  In a
contextual model, the table may
also change when an endpoint category is changed.  Centering a moving table removes its visible row
and column terms, but does not prove that the result is one fixed conditional interaction.

We resolve these ambiguities by taking a \emph{reference-centered operator-valued graph} as the
common mathematical carrier.  Residue positions form the base graph.  Position $i$ carries a
reference-centered categorical fiber $\mathcal H_i^0$, and an edge $j\to i$ carries a map from
categorical contrasts at $j$ to contrasts at $i$.  The framework does not assert one universal
energy or architecture.  Instead, it locates each model in a set of typed audit coordinates:
\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.92\linewidth}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\bfseries}p{0.19\linewidth}p{0.70\linewidth}@{}}
Node variables & What states or features live at each node, and relative to which reference
measure are they centered?\\
Graph and edges & Which directed pairs are present, and what class of map may each edge carry?\\
Available context & Which variables may an edge map observe or depend on?\\
Interaction order & Is the represented dependence pairwise, or does it jointly involve more
than two variables?\\
Dynamics & What state is updated, and by what one-pass or iterative rule?\\
Normalization & What quantity is normalized, conserved, or guaranteed to decrease?\\
Inference & How is the object estimated, which nuisance variation is modeled or
removed, and against what independent endpoint is it validated?
\end{tabular}
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}
These coordinates are deliberately separate.  A sparse graph may carry full-rank categorical blocks; a dense graph may
share only a low-dimensional message family; a perfectly gauged table may lack conditional meaning
if its generator observes the active endpoints; and the same edge operator may be used in a
one-pass predictor or an iterated energy-descent dynamics.

This choice is also an inductive-bias claim.  An architecture restricts the functions that are
easy to represent and learn before any particular data set is observed.  Convolution assumes
locality and translation-related parameter sharing; recurrence assumes a repeatedly applied
transition along an ordered axis; graph computation assumes identifiable entities, relations, and
relation rules that can be reused after the entities are relabeled or recombined.  The last
assumption is the relational inductive bias emphasized by Battaglia et al. as a route toward
combinatorial generalization \cite{battaglia2018relational}.  It is stronger than supplying pair
features to an otherwise unstructured predictor: the representation identifies nodes and edges,
the computation shares update maps across them, and the aggregation respects the irrelevant
ordering of incident edges.  It does not guarantee that a learned relation is causal, physical, or
even distributionally direct.  Those stronger meanings require the additional audit coordinates
introduced here.

The relation between Potts models and protein Transformers is the principal worked example of this
general framework, not a second definition of the framework.  A Potts coupling induces a
background-independent categorical response shared by a protein family.  A protein language model
instead generates an effective response for each sequence background $C$.  Once both are placed in
the same reference geometry, the latter admits the decomposition
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
G_{i\leftarrow j}(C)
=\overline G_{i\leftarrow j}+\delta G_{i\leftarrow j}(C).}
\label{eq:central-thesis}
\end{equation}
The additive Potts limit has zero dynamic residual.  A nonlinear pLM generally has a nonzero
residual; under a fixed product reference it equals higher-order functional mass, while correlated
backgrounds can add nonorthogonal cross terms.  Here the target category is an output-fiber
coordinate, not an assumed model input.  Moreover, a directed response defines a joint
Potts energy only when the two directions satisfy a reciprocity condition.  Thus the comparison is
made in common operator coordinates without asserting equal parameters, equal training laws, or a
universal physical Hamiltonian.

This perspective builds on two direct precedents.  Dauparas et al. placed PSSMs,
pseudolikelihood Potts models, multivariate Gaussian models, and autoencoders in a unified
self-reconstruction framework, exposing the roles of losses, diagonal constraints, and
regularization \cite{dauparas2019}.  Zhang et al. showed that categorical response tensors
extracted from protein language models contain contact-predictive statistics related to interacting
sequence motifs \cite{zhang2024motifs}.  Our aim is an identifiability refinement of these views:
we specify which part of a pair table is an interaction, which model properties survive translation,
and which additional assumptions are required for conditional, probabilistic, physical, or
biological interpretation.

The framework also connects several adjacent lines of work without merging their claims.  Inverse
statistical physics estimates family-level Potts fields from homologous sequences
\cite{morcos2011,ekeberg2013,cocco2018}; Hopfield and RBM models express distributed patterns or
latent motifs \cite{hopfield1982,tubiana2019}; and attention or message passing implements
input-dependent computation \cite{gilmer2017,vaswani2017,ramsauer2021}.  Their common algebraic
primitives do not by themselves establish a common probability law.  Our comparison therefore
occurs after each model has been mapped into declared audit coordinates.

Recent attention-specific results make directionality a testable coordinate rather than only a
graph metaphor.  Bidirectional and autoregressive objectives have been associated respectively
with more symmetric and more directional query--key kernels \cite{saponati2025}; symmetric
dot-product parameterizations can accelerate BERT-style training \cite{courtois2024}; and
Sinkhorn or expected-sliced transport can replace row-stochastic attention by a doubly stochastic
route \cite{sander2022,shahbazi2025}.  Markov-chain and interacting-particle analyses further connect self-attention to
context-conditioned transitions, winner-token behavior, and clustering
\cite{ildiz2024,geshkovski2023}.  These results concern different mathematical objects.  The
framework below distinguishes symmetry of the query--key parameter kernel, symmetry of token
logits, reversibility of the normalized route, and reciprocity of the output response.  None can be
substituted for another without an additional argument.

The book develops the framework in four stages.  First, we define a typed interaction system and its
Potts parameter-gauge quotient, including an exact reference-transport law.  Second, we establish the
Potts--pLM response bridge, its static--dynamic orthogonal decomposition, its product-reference
higher-order identity, and the reciprocity condition for a joint pair energy.  Third, we connect
the linear gauge to exact transport-polytope geometry, distinguish full edge operators from
lossy scalar shadows, separate reversible routing from stationary circulation, and define scalar,
signed, and connection spectra on the appropriate spaces.
Fourth, we specify model-extraction, estimation, architectural
measurability, and falsification audits that state what additional evidence is required at each
semantic level.  Functional ANOVA, Gaussian covariance selection, matrix scaling, and Bayes risk
are classical ingredients; their role here is explicit and delimited.

The scope is a framework rather than a universal equivalence theorem.  We do not claim that every
response operator is a physical contact, that every attention head stores a Potts table, or that a
family MSA is unnecessary for family-specific inference.  The goal is a mathematical coordinate
system in which these claims can be stated, related, and falsified without changing meaning between
statistical physics and machine learning.

The development proceeds from translation rules and the master operator-valued graph to static and
dynamic responses, higher-order and probability geometry, and model correspondences.  A separate
phylogeny chapter then types common ancestry as a generative graph on the MSA sequence axis before
the treatment of estimation and spectra.  Architectural realizability, falsification tests, and
biological endpoints follow.  This order prevents phylogeny from entering only as an informal
post-hoc correction to an already compressed position graph.

The same equation can carry different semantics, and the same word can denote different
equations.  We treat cross-disciplinary translation as a typed map.  Every proposed equivalence
must declare
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{object and sample space},\quad
\text{normalization axis},\quad
\text{conditioning information},\\
\text{gauge or invariance class},\quad
\text{state dynamics},\quad
\text{independent validation claim}.
\end{gathered}}
\label{eq:translation-protocol}
\end{equation}
Algebraic identity of two update formulas is weaker than equality of their probability laws,
physical energies, or biological interpretations.

This dictionary is deliberately asymmetric.  A physical term usually carries more assumptions

\BookSection{Four meanings of interaction}

The word ``interaction'' is the principal translation hazard.  We distinguish:
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item a \emph{computational interaction}: changing one input can alter another node through a
route, message, attention coefficient, or nonzero network response;
\item a \emph{statistical interaction}: a joint or conditional function contains a non-additive
component after a declared lower-order projection;
\item a \emph{physical interaction}: a term belongs to a justified Hamiltonian or effective
energy under declared coarse graining and symmetry assumptions;
\item a \emph{biological interaction}: an intervention establishes a reproducible effect on
structure, fitness, epistasis, or another biological endpoint.
\end{enumerate}
In general none of these statements implies the next.  Transformer attention establishes a
computational route.  A gauge-fixed Potts block establishes a statistical pair component under its
model.  Calling either a physical or biological interaction requires additional evidence.

The same discipline applies to ``direct.''  A nonzero Gaussian precision entry means conditional
dependence under a Gaussian law; a nonzero Potts table is a pair potential in a fitted joint model;
a direct attention edge is one-step execution; and a direct physical contact is a structurally or
experimentally defined relation.

\BookSection{Four meanings of graph}

The word ``graph'' inherits the same ambiguity.  At least four objects must be separated:
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item a \emph{computational route graph}, whose edge $j\to i$ says that an implementation can send
a message from source $j$ to target $i$ in one update;
\item a \emph{statistical support graph}, whose edge says that a declared joint or conditional
model contains a nonzero direct pair component after lower-order terms have been removed;
\item an \emph{operator-valued interaction graph}, whose edge carries the full categorical map
$G_{ij}(a,b)$ rather than only an existence flag or scalar strength;
\item a \emph{physical or biological graph}, whose edges are defined by a stated structural,
energetic, kinetic, or intervention criterion.
\end{enumerate}
The first graph specifies executable communication, the second specifies model factorization, the
third specifies how substitutions act, and the fourth specifies an empirical relation.  Agreement
between them is a hypothesis to test, not a consequence of sharing the same vertex set.

A graph combines support, topology, and the objects carried on its edges.  Its support declares
which effects are treated as direct and which must be mediated by paths; its topology controls degree,
components, cycles, bottlenecks, frustration, and spectral modes.  These collective properties do
not belong to any isolated edge.  Conversely, topology alone is incomplete for categorical
systems: two graphs with identical support and scalar edge norms can carry different singular
directions and make different mutational predictions.  The scalar position graph is consequently
a shadow of the operator-valued graph, not a sufficient physical description.

\BookSection{Softmax is an operation, not a semantics}

Several models use
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{softmax}_{u}(s)_u
=\frac{e^{s_u}}{\sum_v e^{s_v}},
\label{eq:generic-softmax}
\end{equation}
but the index $u$ names different sample spaces.  A Gibbs law may normalize over complete
configurations; a Potts conditional over residue identities at one site; attention over source
positions; and a modern Hopfield update over stored memories.  Reusing the numerical kernel does
not align the corresponding random variables, energies, or graph semantics.

Nevertheless, once the normalized variable has been named, softmax has an exact local
thermodynamic geometry.  For a categorical state $z\in\mathcal Z$, scores $r_z$, and temperature
$\tau>0$, define
\begin{equation}
p_z=\frac{e^{r_z/\tau}}{\sum_{z'}e^{r_{z'}/\tau}},
\qquad E_z=-r_z.
\label{eq:local-gibbs-softmax}
\end{equation}
Then $p$ is the Gibbs distribution of the declared finite-state variable and
\begin{equation}
-\tau\log\sum_z e^{-E_z/\tau}
=\min_{q\in\Delta(\mathcal Z)}
\left\{\sum_z q_zE_z-\tau H(q)\right\}.
\label{eq:softmax-free-energy}
\end{equation}
Thus $\tau$ controls an exact energy--entropy competition: the zero-temperature limit selects
energy minima, while increasing $\tau$ spreads mass over alternatives.  Adding a common constant
to every $r_z$ changes neither $p$ nor any observable energy difference, so the associated energy
coordinate has an additive gauge.  These statements are mathematical identities.  Calling
$\tau$ a physical temperature or $E_z$ a microscopic energy additionally requires calibration,
units, an equilibrium ensemble, and a mechanism that realizes the chosen sample space.

This distinction becomes especially important when the categorical variable is an edge state.
Independent edge-state softmaxes define the factorized graph distribution
\begin{equation}
Q(G\mid x)=\prod_{e\in E_c}q_e(z_e\mid x).
\label{eq:factorized-graph-law}
\end{equation}
This is a collection of local Gibbs coordinates, or a mean-field graph posterior, but not in
general a coupled Gibbs ensemble over graphs.  A global graph law instead has the form
\begin{equation}
P(G\mid x)
=\frac{\exp[-\mathcal H_{\mathrm{graph}}(G;x)/\tau]}
{\sum_{G'}\exp[-\mathcal H_{\mathrm{graph}}(G';x)/\tau]},
\label{eq:global-graph-gibbs-law}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal H_{\mathrm{graph}}$ may couple edges through degree budgets, reciprocity, loop
terms, connectivity, geometry, or higher-order chemical constraints.  Equation
\ref{eq:factorized-graph-law} is exact for Eq.~\ref{eq:global-graph-gibbs-law} only when the graph
energy separates over edges and the admissible graph set itself imposes no coupling.

Static and input-dependent graphs also occupy different statistical roles.  A family-level graph
held fixed while sequences are sampled may be treated as a quenched interaction structure.  A
layer- and sequence-dependent route is an adaptive state of the computation and is only
analogous to an annealed graph.  An equilibrium graph measure additionally requires a coupled
ensemble and a justified energy.  The scientifically heavy claim is the emergence of a
reproducible collective law---for example a support-recovery transition, scaling
collapse, calibrated sparsity frontier, or operator-level agreement under intervention.

An informal claim such as ``attention learns interaction energy'' therefore expands into an audit:
Which variable is normalized?  Is the score part of a compatible joint law?  Which lower-order
components have been removed?  Does the edge remain fixed under the endpoint intervention used to
define interaction?  What observation would distinguish the proposed physical meaning from a
useful predictive route?  Only claims surviving these questions are translated across fields.

\BookSection{A ladder of claims}

The framework separates four increasingly strong predicates:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{projected bivariate component}
\xRightarrow[\text{fixed context}]{\text{endpoint exclusion}}
\text{conditional response}\\
\xRightarrow[\text{one joint law}]{\text{compatibility}}
\text{distributional interaction}
\xRightarrow[\text{matched endpoint}]{\text{independent intervention}}
\text{biological interaction}.
\end{gathered}}
\label{eq:claim-ladder}
\end{equation}
Each arrow is labeled by an additional requirement.  When an upgrade fails, the weaker statement
retains its scope: an attention route can remain computationally
useful without defining a joint interaction law, and a fitted statistical coupling can remain
predictive without establishing biological cause.

The common object must record more than a matrix attached to each pair.  In particular, changing

\begin{definition}[Typed interaction system]
A typed interaction system is a tuple
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak G=
\left(V,\{(\Omega_i,p_i,\mathcal H_i^0)\}_{i\in V},\mathcal C,
E,h,\mathcal K,\mathcal O,\mathcal D,\mathcal N,\mathcal E_{\mathrm{st}},
\mathcal V_{\!\mathrm{ld}}\right).
\label{eq:typed-system}
\end{equation}
Here $V$ is the node set; $(\Omega_i,p_i)$ is the reference probability space at node $i$;
$\mathcal H_i^0\subseteq L_0^2(p_i)$ is the chosen centered feature fiber; $\mathcal C$ is the
context sigma-algebra; $E(C)\subseteq V\times V$ is measurable support; $h(C)\in\bigoplus_i
\mathcal H_i^0$ is the one-body field; and
$\mathcal K_{i\leftarrow j}(C):\mathcal H_j^0\to\mathcal H_i^0$ is the edge map.  The coordinate
$\mathcal O$ records any higher-order maps, $\mathcal D$ the represented state and update rule,
$\mathcal N$ the normalization or Lyapunov functional, $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{st}}$ the estimator and
nuisance model, and $\mathcal V_{\!\mathrm{ld}}$ the independent validation endpoint.
\end{definition}

A model audit is a typed extraction map from model parameters, data, and interventions to
$\mathfrak G$.  Two models may agree in $h$ and $\mathcal K$ while differing in $\mathcal D$,
$\mathcal N$, or $\mathcal E_{\mathrm{st}}$; their agreement is coordinate-wise.  A PSSM, for
example, has a nonzero field $h$ and zero off-diagonal edge operator.  A
Gaussian model can use centered linear features in $L_0^2(p_i)$, whereas the protein models below
use the full centered categorical fiber.

\BookSection{Relational computation and the Graph Network interface}

The Graph Network (GN) formalism isolates a reusable relational computation without prescribing
the scientific semantics of its edges \cite{battaglia2018relational}.  For a graph
$G=(\mathbf u,V,E)$ with directed edge records $(e_k,r_k,s_k)$, one GN block performs
\begin{align}
e'_k&=\phi^e(e_k,v_{r_k},v_{s_k},\mathbf u),
\label{eq:gn-edge-update}\\
\bar e'_i&=\rho^{e\to v}\!\left(\{e'_k:r_k=i\}\right),\qquad
v'_i=\phi^v(v_i,\bar e'_i,\mathbf u),
\label{eq:gn-node-update}\\
\bar e'&=\rho^{e\to u}(E'),\qquad
\bar v'=\rho^{v\to u}(V'),\qquad
\mathbf u'=\phi^u(\mathbf u,\bar e',\bar v').
\label{eq:gn-global-update}
\end{align}
The learnable maps $\phi$ specify relation, node, and global updates.  The reducers $\rho$ are
invariant to an arbitrary enumeration of their input sets, making the complete block equivariant
to node relabeling when the graph and features are relabeled together.  Parameter sharing permits
the same local rule to act on new graph sizes and new combinations of entities.  This is the
architectural basis for combinatorial generalization, not a theorem that such generalization will
occur outside the training distribution.

The typed system in Eq.~\ref{eq:typed-system} refines this interface.  Its support $E(C)$ records
the admissible senders and receivers; $\mathcal K_{i\leftarrow j}(C)$ types the edge update as a
map between declared fibers; and $\mathcal D$ records the aggregation and node-state update.  The
remaining coordinates answer questions that a generic GN block intentionally leaves open:
whether an edge is a route or an identified pair component, whether the updates arise from one
joint probability law, which parameter redundancies preserve observables, how the edge object is
estimated, and what endpoint validates it.  Message-passing networks, Potts fields, and
Transformers can therefore share a relational computation diagram while occupying different
probabilistic and inferential coordinates.  The unification is an interface with typed
translations, not an assertion of semantic identity.

\BookSection{Node fibers and reference geometry}

The word \emph{fiber} carries little mystery here.  At every residue position, take all numerical
functions of residue identity and remove the constant function.  The remaining $(q-1)$ directions
encode comparisons such as ``charged rather than hydrophobic'' or any other centered categorical
contrast.  Calling these local spaces fibers keeps two indices separate: position identifies the
node, while a vector inside the fiber identifies a pattern across residue categories.

Let positions be indexed by $[L]=\{1,\ldots,L\}$ and categorical states by
$[q]=\{1,\ldots,q\}$.  Fix a full-support reference distribution
$p_i\in\Delta_{q-1}$ at each position and write $D_i=\diag(p_i)$.  The node fiber at position $i$
is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H_i=L^2(p_i),
\qquad
\langle f,g\rangle_{p_i}=f^{\mathsf T}D_i g,
\end{equation}
with centered subspace
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H_i^0=\{f:p_i^{\mathsf T}f=0\}.
\label{eq:centered-fiber}
\end{equation}
The total categorical tangent space is the Hilbert direct sum
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H^0=\bigoplus_{i=1}^L\mathcal H_i^0.
\label{eq:direct-sum}
\end{equation}
The position index is the graph base, while amino-acid contrasts form the fiber over each node.
This distinction prevents a position graph from being confused with a flat matrix of categorical
coordinates.

Define the weighted zero-mean projector
\begin{equation}
P_i=I-\mathbf 1p_i^{\mathsf T}.
\label{eq:projector}
\end{equation}
It satisfies $P_i^2=P_i$ and $P_i^{\mathsf T}D_i=D_iP_i$.  In whitened coordinates its Euclidean
form is
\begin{equation}
Q_i=D_i^{1/2}P_iD_i^{-1/2}
=I-\sqrt{p_i}\sqrt{p_i}^{\,\mathsf T}.
\label{eq:whitened-projector}
\end{equation}
Thus the removed constant direction depends on the declared categorical reference.  A uniform
reference, a family marginal, and a model marginal define different geometries and should not be
interchanged silently.  Operationally, $P_i$ takes any residue score vector and subtracts its
$p_i$-weighted mean.  It computes a centered contrast.  Interaction estimation, biological choice
of reference, and physical calibration enter at later stages.

\BookSection{The canonical bivariate component}

For any fixed pair table $T_{ij}\in\R^{q\times q}$, define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{G_{ij}=P_iT_{ij}P_j^{\mathsf T}.}
\label{eq:canonical-gauge}
\end{equation}

Read this operation row by row and column by column: the left projection removes target-only
variation, the right projection removes source-only variation, and the surviving block changes
only when both endpoints vary.  The theorem below establishes that this remainder is unique in the
chosen reference geometry.  Uniqueness is a statement about the decomposition of the table, not
about how the table was generated.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical categorical decomposition]
\label{thm:pair-anova}
Equip pair tables with
\begin{equation}
\langle A,B\rangle_{p_i,p_j}
=\sum_{a,b}p_i(a)p_j(b)A(a,b)B(a,b).
\end{equation}
Every table has a unique orthogonal decomposition
\begin{equation}
T_{ij}(a,b)=c+u(a)+v(b)+G_{ij}(a,b),
\label{eq:pair-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $p_i^{\mathsf T}u=0$, $p_j^{\mathsf T}v=0$, and
\begin{equation}
p_i^{\mathsf T}G_{ij}=0,
\qquad
G_{ij}p_j=0.
\label{eq:zero-marginals}
\end{equation}
The bivariate component is exactly Eq.~\ref{eq:canonical-gauge}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The orthogonal decomposition
$L^2(p_i)=\Span\{\mathbf1\}\oplus\mathcal H_i^0$ and its analogue at $j$ induce four
orthogonal tensor-product subspaces: constant, $i$-only, $j$-only, and centered bivariate.
Applying $P_i$ on the left and $P_j^{\mathsf T}$ on the right selects the last subspace.
Existence and uniqueness follow from orthogonal projection.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Terminology and the binary Ising case.}
Here \emph{gauge} refers only to the additive non-identifiability of a redundant Potts pair-table
parameterization.  We call $G_{ij}$ \emph{reference-centered}, rather than ``gauged,'' to avoid
confusion with the spin-reversal gauge of spin glasses or with a $\mathbb Z_2$ lattice gauge
theory; neither of those symmetries is used here.  For two states with uniform reference, encode
the categories by $s_i,s_j\in\{-1,+1\}$.  Every table has the unique expansion
\begin{equation}
T(s_i,s_j)=c+h_i s_i+h_j s_j+J_{ij}s_is_j,
\qquad
J_{ij}=\frac{T_{++}+T_{--}-T_{+-}-T_{-+}}{4}.
\label{eq:binary-ising-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Double projection removes $c,h_i,$ and $h_j$ and leaves
$G_{ij}(s_i,s_j)=J_{ij}s_is_j$.  Thus the $q=2$ categorical construction extracts the single
Ising coupling coordinate from a redundant binary table.  The ``gauge'' here remains the additive
Potts parameter redundancy, distinct from an Ising gauge field.

The underlying interaction is a gauge class rather than one selected representative.  Define the

\mathcal A_{ij}
=\{T:T(a,b)=c+u(a)+v(b)\}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Gauge quotient and reference transport]
\label{prop:reference-transport}
For fixed full-support references $(p_i,p_j)$, two tables have the same projected block if and only
if they define the same element of $\mathcal Q_{ij}$.  For second full-support references
$(r_i,r_j)$, write $P_i^{[r]}=I-\mathbf1r_i^{\mathsf T}$ and similarly at $j$.  The canonical
representatives obey
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
G_{ij}^{[r]}=P_i^{[r]}G_{ij}^{[p]}P_j^{[r]\mathsf T}.}
\label{eq:reference-transport}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:pair-anova} identifies the kernel of
$T\mapsto P_iTP_j^{\mathsf T}$ with $\mathcal A_{ij}$, proving the quotient statement.  The
projectors for two references satisfy
$P_i^{[r]}P_i^{[p]}=P_i^{[r]}$ and
$P_j^{[p]\mathsf T}P_j^{[r]\mathsf T}=P_j^{[r]\mathsf T}$.  Applying these identities to
$G_{ij}^{[p]}=P_i^{[p]}T_{ij}P_j^{[p]\mathsf T}$ gives
$P_i^{[r]}T_{ij}P_j^{[r]\mathsf T}=G_{ij}^{[r]}$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Reference-invariance of zero context dependence.}
\label{rem:zero-dynamic-reference-invariance}
Let a family of raw tables $T(C)$ be evaluated under one fixed background law, and let
$G^{[p]}(C)$ and $G^{[r]}(C)$ be its representatives under two full-support reference pairs.  If
\begin{equation}
V_{\mathrm{dyn}}^{[p]}
=\E_C\left\|G^{[p]}(C)-\E_CG^{[p]}(C)\right\|_{p_i,p_j}^2,
\end{equation}
and $V_{\mathrm{dyn}}^{[r]}$ is defined analogously, then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{V_{\mathrm{dyn}}^{[p]}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
V_{\mathrm{dyn}}^{[r]}=0.}
\label{eq:zero-dynamic-reference-invariance}
\end{equation}
Indeed, Eq.~\ref{eq:reference-transport} maps a constant representative to a constant
representative, and applying the same transport law in the reverse direction gives the converse.
Thus absence of context dependence is reference independent, although every positive variance,
norm, singular value, and dynamic fraction depends quantitatively on the chosen reference
geometry.  When the denominator in Eq.~\ref{eq:dynamic-fraction} is nonzero, the same equivalence
holds with $V_{\mathrm{dyn}}=0$ replaced by $\rho^{\mathrm{dyn}}=0$.

Thus the gauge class is reference independent, while its centered representative, Hilbert norm,
and singular geometry are reference dependent.  Comparisons across families or contexts must
either use one fixed reference or transport every representative to a declared anchor by
Eq.~\ref{eq:reference-transport}.

\BookSection{From a table to an edge operator}

A centered block becomes an edge only after its input and output are typed.  The next equation feeds
a centered substitution pattern at $j$ into the block and returns a centered response pattern at
$i$.  This is what the operator computes.  Physical action and reciprocity are subsequent empirical
and compatibility questions.

A gauged block induces the directed map
\begin{equation}
(\mathcal K_{i\leftarrow j}f)(a)

=\bigl(G_{i\leftarrow j}D_jf\bigr)(a).
\label{eq:edge-operator}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Gauged tables are fiber maps]
\label{prop:fiber-map}
Equation~\ref{eq:edge-operator} defines a linear map
$\mathcal K_{i\leftarrow j}:\mathcal H_j^0\to\mathcal H_i^0$ that annihilates the constant source
mode.  In whitened coordinates its matrix is
\begin{equation}
\widetilde G_{i\leftarrow j}
=D_i^{1/2}G_{i\leftarrow j}D_j^{1/2},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\|\mathcal K_{i\leftarrow j}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
=\|\widetilde G_{i\leftarrow j}\|_F.
\label{eq:hs-norm}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The left condition in Eq.~\ref{eq:zero-marginals} centers the output, while
$G_{i\leftarrow j}p_j=0$ gives $\mathcal K_{i\leftarrow j}\mathbf1=0$.  Conjugation by the
isometries $f\mapsto D_i^{1/2}f$ and $f\mapsto D_j^{1/2}f$ gives the whitened matrix and preserves
the Hilbert--Schmidt norm.
\end{proof}

For routing coefficients $z_{ij}$, the operator-valued graph is the block operator
\begin{equation}
(\mathbb K_zf)_i
=\sum_{j\ne i}z_{ij}\mathcal K_{i\leftarrow j}f_j.
\label{eq:block-operator}
\end{equation}
Its support, edge rank, and directionality are explicit.  If
$z_{ij}=z_{ji}$ and $G_{j\leftarrow i}=G_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathsf T}$ under compatible
references, $\mathbb K_z$ is self-adjoint.  Predictive sequence models need not satisfy this
reciprocity and may therefore yield genuinely directed block operators.

\BookSection{Potts models as static response fields}

A pairwise Potts model has energy
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm P}(x)
=-\sum_i h_i(x_i)-\frac12\sum_{i\ne j}J_{ij}(x_i,x_j),
\label{eq:potts-energy}
\end{equation}
and single-site conditional
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm P}(X_i=a\mid x_{-i})
=\operatorname{softmax}_{a\in[q]}
\left[h_i(a)+\sum_{j\ne i}J_{ij}(a,x_j)\right].
\label{eq:potts-conditional}
\end{equation}
The tables $J_{ij}$ are fixed after fitting and are shared by all sequences in the family.
Their canonical edge blocks are
\begin{equation}
G_{ij}^{\mathrm P}=P_iJ_{ij}P_j^{\mathsf T}.
\label{eq:potts-block}
\end{equation}
This is the sense in which a Potts interaction graph is static.  The conditional probabilities
still change with $x_{-i}$, but the rule mapping a source category to a target logit contrast does
not.  Static refers to the shared response law, not to constancy of the model output.

\BookSection{Categorical response of a sequence model}

Let $c=x_{[L]\setminus\{i,j\}}$ be a sequence background excluding the endpoints.  Evaluate a
sequence model with target $i$ withheld and source $j$ set to category $b$, and denote its target
logit by
\begin{equation}
T_{i\leftarrow j}^{c}(a,b)
=\ell_i(a\mid X_j=b,C_{ij}=c).
\label{eq:conditional-logit-table}
\end{equation}
The reference-centered categorical response is
\begin{equation}
G_{i\leftarrow j}(c)
=P_iT_{i\leftarrow j}^{c}P_j^{\mathsf T}.
\label{eq:dynamic-response}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, one may subtract any baseline column from $T^c$ before projection.  This gives the
finite substitution table
\begin{equation}
R_{i\leftarrow j}^{c}(a,b)
=\ell_i(a\mid b,c)-\ell_i(a\mid b_0,c),
\label{eq:finite-response}
\end{equation}
with $P_iR^cP_j^{\mathsf T}=G_{i\leftarrow j}(c)$.  The construction is sometimes called a
categorical Jacobian.  More precisely, it is a finite intervention, with an infinitesimal
derivative as its local limit.

\begin{proposition}[Exact recovery of an additive coupling]
\label{prop:exact-recovery}
Suppose the target logits are additive,
\begin{equation}
\ell_i(a\mid x_{-i})
=h_i(a)+\sum_{k\ne i}J_{i\leftarrow k}(a,x_k).
\label{eq:additive-logits}
\end{equation}
Then for every background $c$,
\begin{equation}
G_{i\leftarrow j}(c)
=P_iJ_{i\leftarrow j}P_j^{\mathsf T}.
\label{eq:exact-recovery}
\end{equation}
Thus the gauged response is background independent and exactly recovers the canonical pair
coupling.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In Eq.~\ref{eq:conditional-logit-table}, the field $h_i(a)$ and all source terms with $k\ne j$
depend on $a$ and $c$ but not on $b$.  They are annihilated by right projection.  The remaining
table is $J_{i\leftarrow j}(a,b)$, and left projection removes its target-only gauge component.
\end{proof}

For a nonlinear or higher-order conditional, the same response has the schematic expansion
\begin{equation}
G_{i\leftarrow j}(c)
=G_{ij}^{(2)}
+\sum_{k\notin\{i,j\}}G_{i,j,k}^{(3)}(c_k)
+\sum_{\substack{k<l\\k,l\notin\{i,j\}}}G_{i,j,k,l}^{(4)}(c_k,c_l)+\cdots.
\label{eq:response-order-mixture}
\end{equation}
It is therefore a well-defined \emph{effective} edge at background $c$, assembled from all
statistical and computational paths through which changing
$x_j$ changes the target preference at $i$.

\BookSection{The static--dynamic decomposition}

Fix anchor references $p_i,p_j$ and let $C$ be distributed according to a declared family, model,
or evaluation distribution $\mu_{ij}$.  The gauged blocks form one finite-dimensional Hilbert
space with norm
\begin{equation}
\|G\|_{p_i,p_j}^2
=\sum_{a,b}p_i(a)p_j(b)G(a,b)^2.
\label{eq:weighted-block-norm}
\end{equation}
Assume $\E_C\|G_{i\leftarrow j}(C)\|_{p_i,p_j}^2<\infty$ and define
\begin{equation}
\overline G_{i\leftarrow j}=\E_CG_{i\leftarrow j}(C),
\qquad
\delta G_{i\leftarrow j}(C)
=G_{i\leftarrow j}(C)-\overline G_{i\leftarrow j}.
\label{eq:static-dynamic-split}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Best static approximation and dynamic variance]
\label{thm:static-dynamic}
The mean block $\overline G_{i\leftarrow j}$ is gauged and is the unique minimizer
\begin{equation}
\overline G_{i\leftarrow j}
=\arg\min_{A:\,p_i^{\mathsf T}A=0,\,Ap_j=0}
\E_C\|G_{i\leftarrow j}(C)-A\|_{p_i,p_j}^2.
\label{eq:best-static}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
\E_C\|G_{i\leftarrow j}(C)\|_{p_i,p_j}^2
=\|\overline G_{i\leftarrow j}\|_{p_i,p_j}^2
+\E_C\|\delta G_{i\leftarrow j}(C)\|_{p_i,p_j}^2.
\label{eq:dynamic-pythagoras}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The gauged subspace is linear, so expectation preserves both zero-marginal constraints.  Expanding
$G(C)-A=\delta G(C)+(\overline G-A)$, the cross term vanishes because
$\E_C\delta G(C)=0$.  The resulting Pythagorean identity is minimized uniquely at
$A=\overline G$ and gives Eq.~\ref{eq:dynamic-pythagoras} when $A=0$.
\end{proof}

The orthogonality in Theorem~\ref{thm:static-dynamic} is mean--residual orthogonality in
$L^2(\mu_{ij})$.  It holds for any declared background law $\mu_{ij}$, including a correlated
empirical MSA distribution, because the residual is centered under that same law.  Independence
enters only when the residual is further attributed to mutually orthogonal interaction orders, as
in Corollary~\ref{cor:dynamic-higher-order} below.  Thus correlation changes the order-by-order
interpretation of dynamic variance, while the static--dynamic Pythagorean identity remains exact.

This theorem supplies an operational definition of static and dynamic interaction.  Define
\begin{equation}
\rho_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{dyn}}
=\frac{\E_C\|\delta G_{i\leftarrow j}(C)\|_{p_i,p_j}^2}
{\E_C\|G_{i\leftarrow j}(C)\|_{p_i,p_j}^2},
\qquad 0\leq\rho_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{dyn}}\leq1,
\label{eq:dynamic-fraction}
\end{equation}
whenever the denominator is nonzero.  In finite-precision estimation, the ratio should be reported
only when
\begin{equation}
\E_C\|G_{i\leftarrow j}(C)\|_{p_i,p_j}^2>\tau_G,
\label{eq:dynamic-fraction-threshold}
\end{equation}
for a preregistered absolute or null-calibrated threshold $\tau_G$; otherwise it is undefined, not
zero or one.  This prevents a ratio of two numerical underflow terms on a null edge from appearing
as strong dynamic response.  An additive Potts conditional has
$\rho_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{dyn}}=0$.  A positive value measures background dependence of the effective
response.  Higher-order epistasis, remote homologous patterns, and internal circuits are candidate
sources to be separated by further interventions.

Two background laws expose a genuine interpretation--geometry tradeoff.  With the same endpoint
anchor references, define
\begin{equation}
\rho_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{dyn,prod}}
=\rho_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{dyn}}
\bigg|_{\mu_C=\bigotimes_{k\notin\{i,j\}}p_k},
\qquad
\rho_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{dyn,emp}}
=\rho_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{dyn}}
\bigg|_{\mu_C=\mu_C^{\mathrm{emp}}}.
\label{eq:product-empirical-dynamic-fractions}
\end{equation}
Only the product-background quantity has the exact order-by-order ANOVA interpretation in
Corollary~\ref{cor:dynamic-higher-order}; its independently assembled backgrounds may, however, be
far outside the natural sequence manifold.  The empirical quantity evaluates natural or curated
backgrounds and is therefore closer to the model's training support, although exhaustive endpoint
substitutions can still leave that support.  Correlations destroy order orthogonality, so the
empirical dynamic variance remains a valid mean--residual quantity but not a sum of pure
higher-order masses.  Both should be reported, together with
\begin{equation}
\Delta\rho_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{geom}}
=\rho_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{dyn,emp}}
-\rho_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{dyn,prod}},
\label{eq:dynamic-geometry-gap}
\end{equation}
as a diagnostic of sensitivity to the evaluation geometry rather than as a signed amount of
biological interaction.

The distribution $\mu_{ij}$ is part of the claim.  Averaging over one protein family, all natural
proteins, or model-generated sequences produces different static components.  A pLM can therefore
have learned an amortized dynamic rule, while an MSA estimates a separate family-specific
statistical model against which the response mean can be tested.  Equality between an MSA-fitted
Potts block and the pLM mean is an empirical transfer hypothesis in their common coordinates.  The
family distribution supplies the background law under which that hypothesis is evaluated.

\BookSection{Conditional meaning requires endpoint exclusion}

Equation~\ref{eq:dynamic-response} is a strict conditional component only if the table-generating
context remains fixed while the endpoint states are varied.  Let
$\mathcal C_{ij}=\sigma\{X_k:k\notin\{i,j\}\}$ and allow the reference, raw table, and any route
decision to depend on this cavity.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional gauge under an endpoint-excluding audit]
\label{prop:conditional-gauge}
Require the audit protocol to construct $T_{i\leftarrow j}(C)$, $p_i(C)$, $p_j(C)$, and every route
decision from an endpoint-excluding representation measurable with respect to $\mathcal C_{ij}$;
in particular, it may not reuse a hidden state that previously observed $x_i$ or $x_j$.  If the
conditional references have full support, and the enumerated source category $b$ enters only in the
final endpoint evaluation used to assemble the table, then, almost surely in $C$,
\begin{equation}
G_{i\leftarrow j}(C)
=P_i(C)T_{i\leftarrow j}(C)P_j(C)^{\mathsf T}
\end{equation}
is the unique bivariate contrast relative to
$p_i(\cdot\mid C)p_j(\cdot\mid C)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The protocol condition ensures that, after conditioning on $\mathcal C_{ij}$, the table, route,
and reference measures are fixed while
$(X_i,X_j)$ vary, so Theorem~\ref{thm:pair-anova} applies on each conditional fiber.
\end{proof}

If a hidden representation used to emit the table has already observed $x_i$ or $x_j$, projection
still gives a locally centered table, but not a global conditional decomposition.  This caveat is
especially important when interpreting ordinary attention heads: masking the target and explicitly
enumerating source substitutions defines a cleaner response audit than double centering an
attention-derived table computed from the unmodified endpoints.

When the conditional references in Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional-gauge} depend on $C$, the
blocks inhabit moving fibers.  Transport them into common anchor references
$(\bar p_i,\bar p_j)$ before applying Eq.~\ref{eq:static-dynamic-split}:
\begin{equation}
G_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{anc}}(C)
=P_i^{[\bar p]}G_{i\leftarrow j}^{[p(C)]}(C)P_j^{[\bar p]\mathsf T};
\label{eq:context-anchor-transport}
\end{equation}
then apply the static--dynamic decomposition to $G^{\mathrm{anc}}$.  This convention separates a
change of coordinates from genuine background dependence of the gauge class.

\BookSection{When directed responses define a joint pair energy}

A masked or autoregressive predictor naturally produces directed blocks
$G_{i\leftarrow j}$, whereas a pairwise Hamiltonian uses one reciprocal table for the unordered
pair $\{i,j\}$.  This distinction is another independent coordinate of the framework.

\begin{proposition}[Reciprocity criterion for additive conditionals]
\label{prop:reciprocity}
Consider full-support additive categorical conditionals in one common reference-centered parameterization.  Their
pairwise interaction branch is compatible with a joint Potts energy if and only if
\begin{equation}
G_{i\leftarrow j}=G_{j\leftarrow i}^{\mathsf T}
\qquad\text{for every }i\ne j.
\label{eq:reciprocity}
\end{equation}
When this holds, the common block defines the gauged coupling of the joint energy; one-body fields
are then fixed by the corresponding conditional biases up to a global additive constant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Necessity follows because one joint pair table contributes $J_{ij}(a,b)$ to the conditional at
$i$ and $J_{ij}(a,b)$, with transposed endpoint order, to the conditional at $j$.  Gauge projection
therefore gives Eq.~\ref{eq:reciprocity}.  Conversely, reciprocal gauged blocks can be inserted as
the pair tables of Eq.~\ref{eq:potts-energy}; their single-site conditionals recover the specified
pair branches, while endpoint-only differences are absorbed into the site fields.
\end{proof}

For arbitrary directed responses define the reciprocal and nonintegrable parts
\begin{align}
G_{ij}^{\mathrm{rec}}
&=\frac12\left(G_{i\leftarrow j}+G_{j\leftarrow i}^{\mathsf T}\right),\\
G_{ij}^{\mathrm{irr}}
&=\frac12\left(G_{i\leftarrow j}-G_{j\leftarrow i}^{\mathsf T}\right).
\label{eq:reciprocal-irreversible}
\end{align}
Only the first is directly compatible with an undirected pair energy.  The second measures failure
of pairwise joint integrability in the chosen response coordinates.  A thermodynamic
irreversibility interpretation additionally requires a dynamical model.

\BookSection{From operator-valued graphs to hypergraphs}

A graph retains target--source maps
$\mathcal K_{i\leftarrow j}:\mathcal H_j^0\to\mathcal H_i^0$.  A general conditional model also
contains hyperedge maps
\begin{equation}
\mathcal K_{i\leftarrow A}:
\bigotimes_{j\in A}\mathcal H_j^0\longrightarrow\mathcal H_i^0,
\qquad |A|\geq2.
\label{eq:hyperedge-map}
\end{equation}
For a fixed background outside $\{i\}\cup N$, let $E_u$ average over $x_u$ under a positive
product reference and let $P_u=I-E_u$.  For $B\subseteq\{i\}\cup N$, define
\begin{equation}
\Pi_B=
\left(\prod_{u\in B}P_u\right)
\left(\prod_{v\notin B}E_v\right).
\label{eq:hierarchical-projector}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Interaction hierarchy and pair-truncation error]
\label{thm:pair-truncation}
For $T\in L^2(\bigotimes_{u\in\{i\}\cup N}p_u)$, the operators $\{\Pi_B\}$ are mutually
orthogonal projections that sum to the identity, and $T=\sum_B\Pi_BT$ uniquely.  Let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal I_i=\sum_{\emptyset\ne A\subseteq N}T_{i,A},
\qquad
T_{i,A}=\Pi_{\{i\}\cup A}T.
\end{equation}
For the pairwise truncation $\mathcal I_i^{(1)}=\sum_{j\in N}T_{i,\{j\}}$,
\begin{equation}
\|\mathcal I_i-\mathcal I_i^{(1)}\|^2
=\sum_{A\subseteq N:\,|A|\geq2}\|T_{i,A}\|^2.
\label{eq:pair-truncation}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The coordinate-wise averaging operators are commuting self-adjoint idempotents, and
$P_u=I-E_u$ is the orthogonal complement of $E_u$.  Expanding
$\prod_u(E_u+P_u)=I$ gives the decomposition.  Distinct components contain a factor
$E_uP_u=0$ and are orthogonal.  Equation~\ref{eq:pair-truncation} is Pythagoras applied to the
omitted terms.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Dynamic variance equals higher-order response mass]
\label{cor:dynamic-higher-order}
Let $N\subseteq[L]\setminus\{i\}$ contain $j$ and define the centered, vector-valued target score
\begin{equation}
\Phi(x_N)=P_i\ell_i(\mathord\cdot\mid x_N)
\in\mathcal H_i^0,
\qquad
\Phi\in L^2\!\left(\bigotimes_{u\in N}p_u;\mathcal H_i^0\right).
\label{eq:vector-valued-target-score}
\end{equation}
For $B\subseteq N$, let
$\Pi_B^N=(\prod_{u\in B}P_u)(\prod_{v\in N\setminus B}E_v)$ act on the source coordinates of this
Hilbert-valued function, and write $\Phi_B=\Pi_B^N\Phi$.  Under the product reference, the audited
response satisfies
\begin{align}
G_{i\leftarrow j}(C)&=\sum_{B\subseteq N:\,j\in B}\Phi_B,\\
\overline G_{i\leftarrow j}&=\Phi_{\{j\}},\\
\E_C\|\delta G_{i\leftarrow j}(C)\|_{p_i,p_j}^2
&=\sum_{B\subseteq N:\,B\supsetneq\{j\}}\|\Phi_B\|^2.
\label{eq:dynamic-higher-order}
\end{align}
Thus dynamic response variance is exactly the total functional-ANOVA mass of components containing
the audited source and at least one additional source coordinate.  The target category remains an
output coordinate in $\mathcal H_i^0$; it is not assumed to be an observed model input.
The norms on the right are the Bochner $L^2$ norms over the indicated source coordinates and the
target fiber.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The commuting source-coordinate projections extend to the Bochner space in
Eq.~\ref{eq:vector-valued-target-score} and remain mutually orthogonal.  Applying $P_j$ retains
exactly the components whose source set contains $j$; $P_i$ has already centered the output fiber.
Averaging over $C=X_{N\setminus\{j\}}$ retains only $\Phi_{\{j\}}$.  Orthogonality of the remaining
Hilbert-valued components gives the squared-norm identity.
\end{proof}

The theorem gives Eq.~\ref{eq:response-order-mixture} a precise interpretation.  A dynamic edge
response is a section through the higher-order expansion with all other coordinates fixed.  Its
background variance is therefore an exact higher-order mass under the product reference.  Under a
correlated empirical background distribution the components can lose orthogonality and acquire
cross terms; the product reference or a declared conditional geometry is part of the theorem.
Even in the orthogonal case, resolving individual hyperedges requires a designed
multi-substitution experiment beyond their total variance.

\begin{remark}[Mean orthogonality versus order orthogonality]
Theorem~\ref{thm:static-dynamic} and Corollary~\ref{cor:dynamic-higher-order} use two different
orthogonalities.  The first centers one random operator around its mean and requires no coordinate
independence.  The second decomposes that operator by subsets of sequence coordinates and uses a
product reference.  If the same order-indexed components are evaluated under a correlated law
$\mu$, then in general
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\E_{\mu}\left\|\sum_{B\supsetneq\{j\}}\Phi_B(C_B)\right\|^2
&=\sum_{B\supsetneq\{j\}}\E_{\mu}\|\Phi_B(C_B)\|^2\\
&\quad+\sum_{\substack{B,D\supsetneq\{j\}\\B\ne D}}
\E_{\mu}\langle \Phi_B(C_B),\Phi_D(C_D)\rangle,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:correlated-anova-cross-terms}
\end{equation}
and the final sum records cross terms induced by the correlated reference geometry.  Dynamic
variance remains measurable, but it is no longer identifiable with a simple sum of order-specific
ANOVA masses.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Nonlinear composition can create higher order]
\label{prop:nonlinear-nonclosure}
Separately centered pair messages can generate higher-order components after nonlinear
aggregation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For independent Rademacher sources $x_j,x_k\in\{-1,1\}$ under the uniform product reference and a
nonzero target contrast $P_i u$,
\begin{equation}
(P_i u)(a)(x_j+x_k)^2
=2(P_i u)(a)+2(P_i u)(a)x_jx_k.
\end{equation}
After assigning the first term to the target-only component, the second is a nonzero
target--source--source interaction.
\end{proof}

Consequently, pairwise first-layer messages can produce a globally higher-order deep network.  Its
final response must be decomposed at the output, or its higher-order residual measured by
multi-background and multi-substitution audits.

\BookSection{Support, categorical rank, and order are independent}

For a nonzero whitened block, write
\begin{equation}
\widetilde G_{ij}
=\sum_{r=1}^{q-1}\sigma_{ijr}u_{ijr}v_{ijr}^{\mathsf T}.
\label{eq:block-svd}
\end{equation}
Graph support counts which position pairs are represented.  Categorical rank describes the number
of substitution-contrast directions retained on one edge.  Interaction order distinguishes edges
from hyperedges.  These are independent coordinates: a sparse graph may have
full-rank categorical blocks, while a dense graph may share only a low-dimensional message family.

For a relative tolerance $\epsilon$, an error-controlled edge rank is
\begin{equation}
r_{ij}(\epsilon)=\min\left\{r:
\frac{\sum_{s>r}\sigma_{ijs}^2}{\sum_s\sigma_{ijs}^2}\leq\epsilon\right\}.
\label{eq:approx-rank}
\end{equation}
Low rank is therefore a measurable compression hypothesis independent of graph sparsity.

\BookSection{The scalar position graph is a shadow}

The common scalar compression is
\begin{equation}
M_{ij}=\|G_{ij}\|_{p_i,p_j}
=\|D_i^{1/2}G_{ij}D_j^{1/2}\|_F.
\label{eq:scalar-shadow}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Loss and order of scalar compression]
\label{prop:scalar-loss}
The map $G_{ij}\mapsto M_{ij}$ discards sign, singular directions, and categorical action.  In
addition, projection must precede the norm: for any nonzero $v\in\R^q$, the one-body table
$T=\mathbf1v^{\mathsf T}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
P_iTP_j^{\mathsf T}=0,
\qquad
\|T\|_{p_i,p_j}=\|v\|_{p_j}>0.
\label{eq:one-body-weighted-norm}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Norms are invariant under sign and orthogonal changes of singular vectors, so the compression is
many-to-one.  The final statement follows from $P_i\mathbf1=0$ and direct evaluation of the
weighted norm:
$\sum_{a,b}p_i(a)p_j(b)v(b)^2=\sum_bp_j(b)v(b)^2$.
\end{proof}

\BookSection{From tangent coordinates to finite pair laws}

The linear gauge identifies a function-space component.  Exact preservation of prescribed
one-site marginals after exponentiation and normalization additionally requires the transportation
polytope
\begin{equation}
\mathcal U(p_i,p_j)
=\{Q\geq0:Q\mathbf1=p_i,\ Q^{\mathsf T}\mathbf1=p_j\}.
\label{eq:transport-polytope}
\end{equation}
It has dimension $(q-1)^2$, matching the dimension of the categorical tangent-product space.

\begin{equation}
R_{ij}
=D_i^{-1/2}(Q_{ij}-p_ip_j^{\mathsf T})D_j^{-1/2}
\label{eq:standardized-residual}
\end{equation}
has zero constant modes and rank at most $q-1$.  It is the correspondence-analysis coordinate of

\BookSection{Matrix scaling on the gauge quotient}

Given a finite score table $S_{ij}$, let $K_{ij}=\exp S_{ij}$ elementwise.  For strictly positive
$K_{ij},p_i,p_j$, matrix scaling supplies positive vectors $u_{ij},v_{ij}$ such that
\begin{equation}
Q_{ij}=\diag(u_{ij})K_{ij}\diag(v_{ij})
\label{eq:sinkhorn-coupling}
\end{equation}
has the prescribed marginals \cite{sinkhorn1964,darroch1972,csiszar1975,cuturi2013}.  The coupling
is unique, while $(u,v)$ are unique up to reciprocal scaling.

\begin{proposition}[Exact gauge invariance of matrix scaling]
\label{prop:sinkhorn-gauge}
Let $\mathscr S_{p_i,p_j}(S)$ denote the coupling obtained by scaling $\exp S$ to marginals
$(p_i,p_j)$.  For arbitrary vectors $a,b$ and scalar $c$,
\begin{equation}
\mathscr S_{p_i,p_j}
\left(S+a\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}+\mathbf1b^{\mathsf T}+c\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\right)
=\mathscr S_{p_i,p_j}(S).
\label{eq:sinkhorn-gauge}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the exact nonlinear map $S\mapsto Q$ factors through the same quotient
$\mathcal Q_{ij}$ as the linear Potts parameter-gauge quotient.  The induced map is a bijection
from $\mathcal Q_{ij}$ onto the relative interior
$\operatorname{relint}\mathcal U(p_i,p_j)$, consisting of couplings with strictly positive entries.
Finite score tables do not reach the boundary of the transport polytope.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exponentiating the added row, column, and constant terms only left- and right-scales $K$ by
positive diagonal matrices and a positive scalar.  These factors are absorbed into the Sinkhorn
vectors.  Uniqueness of the coupling with the prescribed marginals gives
Eq.~\ref{eq:sinkhorn-gauge}.
Conversely, for any strictly positive $Q\in\mathcal U(p_i,p_j)$, choosing $S=\log Q$ returns $Q$
without nontrivial scaling.  If two finite score tables return the same positive coupling, their
exponentials differ only by the positive diagonal scaling absorbed by Sinkhorn, so their logarithms
differ by row, column, and constant terms.  This proves bijectivity on the quotient.
\end{proof}

Along
$K_\epsilon=\exp(\epsilon S_{ij})$ around independence,
\begin{equation}
\left.\frac{dQ_{ij}(\epsilon)}{d\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}
=D_i(P_iS_{ij}P_j^{\mathsf T})D_j.
\label{eq:sinkhorn-linearization}
\end{equation}
Thus the reference-centered representative is both the tangent-space linearization of exact
marginal-preserving pair geometry and a linear coordinate on the same gauge quotient through which
the exact Sinkhorn map factors.

\BookSection{Local pair laws and global compatibility}

The exact conditional $q_{i\mid j}(a\mid b)=Q_{ij}(a,b)/p_j(b)$ is normalized for each $b$, and
averaging over $b\sim p_j$ returns $p_i$.  Its mutual information
\begin{equation}
I_{ij}=\operatorname{KL}(Q_{ij}\|p_ip_j^{\mathsf T})
\end{equation}
is an exact nonnegative scalar association.  Near independence,
\begin{equation}
I_{ij}=\frac12\|R_{ij}\|_F^2+O(\|R_{ij}\|_F^3),
\label{eq:mi-local-geometry}
\end{equation}
with a remainder controlled only when the smallest marginal probability stays away from zero.
Rare and unsupported categories therefore require an explicit smoothing convention.

This construction separates three levels:
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item $P_iS_{ij}P_j^{\mathsf T}$ is a marginal-orthogonal logit contrast;
\item $Q_{ij}\in\mathcal U(p_i,p_j)$ is one exact pair law with prescribed marginals;
\item a collection of pair laws is globally probabilistic only if it lies in the marginal polytope
of one compatible sequence distribution.
\end{enumerate}
The third property is a global compatibility constraint beyond a sparse pair layer or pairwise
Sinkhorn scaling.  Marginal log-linear, discrete dependence, and minimum-information models provide global alternatives but
introduce a separate estimation problem \cite{bergsma2002,geenens2020,sei2024}.

\BookSection{Static fields, Gaussian conditionals, and low-rank patterns}

A PSSM retains only the one-body branch $h_i(a)$ and therefore occupies the field coordinate with
zero off-diagonal edge operator.  A Potts model adds static full categorical edge blocks.  We first
retain the model-specific coordinates before asking which of them can be collapsed into a shared
reconstruction diagram.

For a nondegenerate Gaussian vector with covariance $\Sigma$ and precision
$\Omega=\Sigma^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}
\Omega_{ij}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
X_i\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\perp}X_j
\mid X_{[L]\setminus\{i,j\}}.
\label{eq:gaussian-ci}
\end{equation}
This is a distributional theorem under a Gaussian law \cite{dempster1972}.  Leading-mode deletion
is a separate spectral operation, while a Gaussian approximation to one-hot protein variables
still requires reference-centered Potts coordinates for categorical comparison.

A classical Hopfield--Potts restriction factorizes the static coupling as
\begin{equation}
J_{ij}(a,b)
=\sum_{\mu=1}^{p}\lambda_\mu
\xi_{i\mu}(a)\xi_{j\mu}(b).
\label{eq:hopfield-potts}
\end{equation}
It is a low-rank parameterization of a pairwise Potts energy \cite{hopfield1982,cocco2013}.
Attention belongs to a separate state-dependent routing construction.  Positive and negative pattern weights select different operator
directions, and gauge projection is still required.  At the pairwise level one may test
\begin{equation}
\mathbb K^{(2)}
=\mathbb K_{\mathrm{LR}}+\mathbb K_{\mathrm{sp}},
\label{eq:lowrank-sparse}
\end{equation}
where the two terms represent distributed collective modes and localized typed constraints.  This
decomposition is a model hypothesis: low-rank and sparse classes overlap, so recovery needs
declared penalties, incoherence assumptions, synthetic ground truth, or independent interventions.

\BookSection{Transformer computation and interaction graphs}

A self-attention layer is a message-passing operation whose nodes are tokens.  Its candidate
support is usually the complete directed graph (or the graph allowed by a mask), whereas its
effective weighted route is generated anew from the current representations.  For head $h$,
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{ij}^{(h)}(x)
=\operatorname{softmax}_{j}
\left(\frac{Q_i^{(h)}(x)^{\mathsf T}K_j^{(h)}(x)}{\sqrt{d_h}}\right),
\qquad
y_i=\sum_{j,h}\alpha_{ij}^{(h)}B_hx_j.
\label{eq:attention}
\end{equation}
In this computational sense a Transformer instantiates a special dynamic GN block: the same
query--key and value rules are shared across token pairs, the positional aggregation is
permutation-respecting before positional encodings or masks are supplied, and the effective edge
weights depend on the current sequence and layer
\cite{battaglia2018relational,gilmer2017,velickovic2018,vaswani2017}.  Training therefore learns a
shared graph-generating rule $X\mapsto P_h(X)$.  The resulting route is input-specific, while the
statistical operator in Eq.~\ref{eq:edge-operator} is obtained through a separate output audit.

There are at least four non-equivalent symmetry questions inside Eq.~\ref{eq:attention}.  For a
head with shared linear query--key maps and no additional position-dependent score transform, let
$X$ collect the current token states and set
\begin{equation}
C_h=W_Q^{(h)}W_K^{(h)\mathsf T},\qquad
S_h(X)=\frac{XC_hX^{\mathsf T}}{\sqrt{d_h}},\qquad
P_h(X)=\operatorname{softmax}_{\mathrm{row}}S_h(X),
\label{eq:attention-symmetry-levels}
\end{equation}
one may ask whether (i) $C_h$ is symmetric, (ii) the token logit matrix $S_h(X)$ is symmetric,
(iii) the normalized route $P_h(X)$ is reversible, or (iv) the audited categorical response obeys
Eq.~\ref{eq:reciprocity}.  Symmetry of $C_h$ implies symmetry of $S_h(X)$ before an asymmetric
mask, relative-position bias, or position-dependent query--key transform is applied, but row
normalization generally gives $P_{ij}\ne P_{ji}$.  Stationary flux, rather than entrywise symmetry,
determines whether this asymmetry carries nonreciprocal circulation.  Indeed, if
$K_{ij}=\exp S_{ij}=K_{ji}$, $z_i=\sum_jK_{ij}$, and
\begin{equation}
P_{ij}=\frac{K_{ij}}{z_i},\qquad
\pi_i=\frac{z_i}{\sum_k z_k},
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
\pi_iP_{ij}=\pi_jP_{ji}.
\label{eq:softmax-detailed-balance}
\end{equation}
Thus a symmetric score followed by row-softmax is generally asymmetric in Euclidean coordinates
but reversible in its stationary geometry.  An asymmetric query--key kernel or mask can break
this property, although either can also produce accidental reversibility on a particular input.

This distinction sharpens recent empirical results.  Saponati et al. analyze symmetry and column
dominance primarily in $C_h$, reporting that bidirectional objectives favor symmetric structure
whereas autoregressive objectives favor directionality; symmetric initialization improves several
encoder-language training runs, while the reported vision gains are not significant and
large-scale behavior remains open \cite{saponati2025}.  Courtois et al. similarly improve
BERT-style efficiency with a symmetric dot-product parameterization \cite{courtois2024}.  Neither
result addresses the normalized token route or the end-to-end substitution response directly.
Doubly stochastic attention, in turn, balances incoming and outgoing mass while allowing
asymmetric directed cycles.  Sinkformers implement
this constraint by iterative matrix scaling, while ESPFormer uses parallel expected-sliced
transport and reports improvements across several modalities \cite{sander2022,shahbazi2025}.
Marginal balance and detailed balance remain two independently testable properties.

At fixed attention coefficients, the linear value-path map is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_{i\leftarrow j}
=\sum_{h=1}^H\alpha_{ij}^{(h)}B_h.
\label{eq:frozen-attention-map}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Shared span of a frozen attention layer]
\label{prop:attention-span}
Every map in Eq.~\ref{eq:frozen-attention-map} lies in the shared subspace
$\Span\{B_1,\ldots,B_H\}$.  Dense connectivity therefore distributes a shared $H$-dimensional
operator family across its edges.
\end{proposition}

The complete input--output response also contains derivatives or finite differences through
queries, keys, earlier layers, residual paths, and nonlinear blocks.  This is why the categorical
output audit in Eq.~\ref{eq:dynamic-response} is the proper model-level object for comparison with
a Potts block.  The attention map supplies the route graph.  Route and response graphs should
therefore be reported separately: the first describes where a layer reads, while the second
describes how a source substitution changes the model's categorical prediction.

\BookSection{MSA Transformer as amortized family inference}

MSA Transformer makes the family sample an explicit neural state rather than compressing it into
fixed counts before the network \cite{rao2021}.  For an alignment with $R$ homologous sequences and
$L$ positions, a layer acts on
\begin{equation}
H_f\in\R^{R\times L\times d}.
\label{eq:msa-transformer-state}
\end{equation}
Column attention compares alignment rows at one fixed position,
\begin{equation}
A^{\mathrm{col}}_{i,rs}
=\operatorname{softmax}_{s}
\left(\frac{q_{ri}^{\mathsf T}k_{si}}{\sqrt d}\right),
\qquad
y_{ri}^{\mathrm{col}}=\sum_sA^{\mathrm{col}}_{i,rs}v_{si},
\label{eq:msa-column-attention}
\end{equation}
whereas tied row attention aggregates evidence across homologues to produce one position route
shared by all rows, schematically
\begin{equation}
S^{\mathrm{row}}_{ij}
=\frac1R\sum_{r=1}^R\frac{q_{ri}^{\mathsf T}k_{rj}}{\sqrt d},
\qquad
A^{\mathrm{row}}_{ij}=\operatorname{softmax}_{j}S^{\mathrm{row}}_{ij},
\qquad
y_{ri}^{\mathrm{row}}=\sum_jA^{\mathrm{row}}_{ij}v_{rj}.
\label{eq:msa-tied-row-attention}
\end{equation}
Thus the native computation contains two graph families: an $R$-node sequence graph for each
position and an $L$-node position graph conditioned on the complete MSA.  Axial factorization
reduces full attention over $RL$ cells from $O(R^2L^2)$ to
$O(LR^2+RL^2)$ per layer.  Alternating the axes still permits information to travel from
$(r,i)$ to $(s,j)$ through two-hop paths, but it is an architectural factorization rather than an
exact equality with unrestricted joint attention.

The cross-family commonality is most cleanly expressed hierarchically.  Let $\Theta_f$ denote the
family-specific constraint system---fields, typed couplings, structural organization, and
functional restrictions---and let $\mathcal T_f$ denote phylogeny and sampling.  A schematic
population model is
\begin{equation}
\Theta_f\sim P_\star(\Theta),
\qquad
\mathcal M_f\sim p(\mathcal M\mid\Theta_f,\mathcal T_f).
\label{eq:family-hierarchical-model}
\end{equation}
Families do not share one coupling graph.  They share recurring amino-acid response motifs,
structural graph organization, conservation and compensation patterns, and the statistical task of
separating constraint from finite depth, gaps, subfamilies, and common ancestry.  Training across
many alignments learns an amortized inference rule
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal A_\theta:\mathcal M_f
\longmapsto
\widehat\Theta_f\ \text{or directly to family-conditioned predictions}.}
\label{eq:msa-amortized-family-map}
\end{equation}
Here $\widehat\Theta_f$ is a conceptual audit coordinate, not necessarily an explicit stored model
variable.  The current MSA supplies family evidence; the shared parameters supply a cross-family
prior over how such evidence should be interpreted.

This gives a precise intermediate position between DCA and a single-sequence pLM:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Potts / DCA:}&\quad
\mathcal M_f\xrightarrow{\text{family optimization}}\widehat J_f,\\
\text{MSA Transformer:}&\quad
(\theta_\star,\mathcal M_f)\xrightarrow{\text{forward inference}}
G_f(C;\mathcal M_f),\\
\text{single-sequence pLM:}&\quad
(\theta_\star,x)\xrightarrow{\text{amortized inference}}G(C;x).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:three-family-inference-regimes}
\end{equation}
The first estimates one static family field, the second combines an explicit family sample with a
cross-family learned estimator, and the third relies on information already amortized into model
parameters.  Tied row attention can contain contact-predictive statistics, but its scalar route is
not a $q\times q$ Potts block.  Column attention is likewise a normalized route over homologues, not
a phylogenetic tree.  Output substitution audits, clade-aware nulls, and row/column interventions
are required to distinguish reusable family constraints from ancestry and sampling shortcuts.

\BookSection{Softmax over different sample spaces}

The Potts conditional in Eq.~\ref{eq:potts-conditional} normalizes over residue identities at one
position.  Attention in Eq.~\ref{eq:attention} normalizes over source positions.  Modern Hopfield
retrieval normalizes over stored memories,
\begin{equation}
q^{\mathrm{new}}
=\Xi\operatorname{softmax}_{\mu}(\beta\Xi^{\mathsf T}q),
\label{eq:modern-hopfield-update}
\end{equation}
and can be derived from the Lyapunov function
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{MH}}(q)
=\frac12\|q\|_2^2
-\frac1\beta\log\sum_\mu e^{\beta\xi_\mu^{\mathsf T}q}+C
\label{eq:modern-hopfield-energy}
\end{equation}
under the assumptions of the modern Hopfield construction \cite{ramsauer2021}.  One attention
readout matches this update when keys and returned values are tied or compatibly mapped.  Untied
values, changing memories, residual connections, and feed-forward layers generally define a
different dynamics whose energy descent must be established separately.

The bridge between latent-variable models and modern Hopfield retrieval becomes exact after the
hidden sample space is specified.  Replace the independent Bernoulli hidden vector of an RBM by one
categorical memory variable $Z\in[M]$ and define
\begin{equation}
E(q,Z=\mu)=\frac12\|q\|_2^2-\xi_\mu^{\mathsf T}q.
\label{eq:categorical-memory-energy}
\end{equation}
Its posterior is
\begin{equation}
P(Z=\mu\mid q)
=\operatorname{softmax}_{\mu}(\beta\Xi^{\mathsf T}q),
\label{eq:categorical-memory-posterior}
\end{equation}
and marginalizing $Z$ gives Eq.~\ref{eq:modern-hopfield-energy} up to a constant.  The essential
distinction is therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{Bernoulli RBM:}&\quad
\log\sum_{r\in\{0,1\}^M}e^{r^{\mathsf T}s}
=\sum_\mu\log(1+e^{s_\mu}),\\
\text{modern Hopfield:}&\quad
\log\sum_{\mu=1}^M e^{\beta\xi_\mu^{\mathsf T}q}.
\end{aligned}}
\label{eq:hidden-sample-spaces}
\end{equation}
The first permits multiple motifs to be active independently; the second makes memories compete on
one simplex.  Transformer attention applies the same categorical-memory posterior separately at
each target, but its memories are generated by the current sequence rather than stored as fixed
parameters.  Multi-head attention repeats retrieval in several learned memory spaces.

The three energy-like quantities must therefore remain distinct:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
E_{\mathrm{Potts}}(x)
\ne E_{\mathrm{Hopfield}}(q)
\ne-\log\alpha_{ij}.}
\label{eq:three-energies}
\end{equation}
They score a sequence configuration, a retrieval state, and one normalized routing coordinate,
respectively.

\BookSection{Latent factors and interaction order}

For binary hidden variables $r_\mu$, an RBM energy can be written
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{RBM}}(x,r)
=-\sum_i g_i(x_i)-\sum_\mu c_\mu r_\mu
-\sum_{i,\mu}W_{i\mu}(x_i)r_\mu.
\end{equation}
Marginalizing the hidden layer gives
\begin{equation}
\log\widetilde P_{\mathrm{RBM}}(x)
=\sum_i g_i(x_i)
+\sum_\mu\log\left[1+\exp\left(c_\mu+\sum_iW_{i\mu}(x_i)\right)\right].
\label{eq:rbm-marginal}
\end{equation}
The nonlinear softplus generates one-body, pairwise, and higher-order terms.  Its weak-coupling
quadratic component is low rank across positions; a Potts approximation additionally requires a
negligible higher-order remainder.  Protein RBMs can therefore represent extended
motifs without identifying every pair touched by a hidden unit as a direct contact
\cite{hinton2002,tubiana2019}.

The useful unification is consequently typed: Potts couplings, low-rank Hopfield--Potts patterns,
RBM-induced pair components, and pLM substitution responses can all be projected into the same
edge-operator coordinates.  Equality in those coordinates is then an empirical hypothesis tested
after the shared matrix multiplication or softmax has been typed.

\BookChapter{Phylogeny as Tree-Constrained Transport}

Phylogeny is not merely a correction applied after an MSA statistic has been computed.  It is a
distinct generative graph on the sequence axis.  Its vertices include latent ancestors, its edges
carry evolutionary transition kernels, and its shared topology makes the observed alignment rows
dependent.  This graph must be separated from the residue-position graph, whose edges describe
statistical or functional relations within a sequence, and from an MSA Transformer's row route,
which is generated between observed sequences by a learned computation.  The distinction supplies
the missing type information behind the informal statement that an MSA contains both constraint
and common ancestry.

\BookSection{Why a tree is a special computational structure}

Let $T=(V,E)$ be a tree with observed leaves $\mathcal L$, latent internal vertices
$\mathcal I=V\setminus\mathcal L$, and a root chosen to orient the computation.  A tree is not only
a sparse graph.  It has no cycles, every pair of vertices is joined by one path, and deleting any
edge separates it into exactly two components.  If $p$ is the parent of $u$, let $D_u$ be the
descendant component containing $u$ after $(p,u)$ is deleted.  The Markov property on the tree gives
\begin{equation}
X_{D_u}
\mathrel{\perp\!\!\!\perp}
X_{V\setminus(D_u\cup\{p\})}
\mid X_p .
\label{eq:tree-separator-independence}
\end{equation}
Thus the single categorical variable $X_p$ separates the complete descendant subtree from the
rest of the model.  In graphical-model language the separator has size one and the tree has
treewidth one.

This separator property is the source of the algorithm.  All observations below $u$ can be
summarized for the rest of the tree by the $q$ numbers
\begin{equation}
m_{u\to p}(a)
=P\!\left(
x_{\mathcal L\cap D_u}\mid X_p=a
\right),
\qquad a\in[q].
\label{eq:phylo-subtree-message}
\end{equation}
The size of this interface does not grow with the number of descendants.  Dynamic programming,
belief propagation, variable elimination, and contraction of a tree tensor network are four
descriptions of this same compression.  On a graph with cycles, eliminating a node generally
creates larger separators and higher-order intermediate factors; exact cost is then governed by
treewidth rather than vertex count alone.

\BookSection{The classical recursion is exact message passing on a tree}

For one aligned site, let $Q$ be a continuous-time substitution generator on $q$ categorical
states.  An oriented edge $e=(p,u)$ of length $t_e$ carries the Markov operator
\begin{equation}
M_e=\exp(t_eQ),
\qquad
M_e(a,b)=P(X_u=b\mid X_p=a).
\label{eq:phylo-edge-kernel}
\end{equation}
Let $\phi_u(b)$ be the observation factor: it is one at an unobserved internal vertex and is the
indicator or ambiguity distribution of the observed category at a leaf.  Conditional independence
between child subtrees gives the Felsenstein message
\begin{equation}
m_{u\to p}(a)
=\sum_{b=1}^{q}
M_{(p,u)}(a,b)\,
\phi_u(b)
\prod_{v\in\operatorname{ch}(u)}m_{v\to u}(b).
\label{eq:felsenstein-message}
\end{equation}
The site likelihood is
\begin{equation}
p(x_{\mathrm{leaf}}\mid T,Q,t)
=\sum_{a=1}^{q}\pi(a)
\phi_r(a)
\prod_{v\in\operatorname{ch}(r)}
m_{v\to r}(a),
\label{eq:phylo-site-likelihood}
\end{equation}
where $r$ is the root and $\pi$ its state distribution.  For a fixed tree this sum--product
recursion evaluates the likelihood in $O(|V|q^2)$ time per site rather than summing explicitly over
all ancestral assignments \cite{felsenstein1981}.  Its age is therefore not evidence of a missing
neural replacement: it is already the exact dynamic program for the declared tree model.

Equation~\ref{eq:felsenstein-message} also locates phylogeny inside the typed framework.  The edge
map $M_{(p,u)}$ transports a categorical function from the child fiber to the parent fiber, while
the product at $u$ combines conditionally independent descendant evidence.  This is a Graph
Network on an augmented vertex set with latent nodes, but it is not a residue interaction graph
and not a message-passing layer over only the observed MSA rows.

\BookSection{Inference on a tree is not inference of a tree}

Two computational problems are often compressed into the phrase "phylogenetic inference":
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{inference on a tree:}&\quad
(T,Q,t)\longmapsto p(x_{\mathcal L}\mid T,Q,t),\\
\text{inference of a tree:}&\quad
x_{\mathcal L}\longmapsto
\widehat T,\widehat Q,\widehat t
\ \text{or}\ p(T,Q,t\mid x_{\mathcal L}).
\end{aligned}}
\label{eq:on-tree-versus-of-tree}
\end{equation}
The first problem uses Eq.~\ref{eq:felsenstein-message} and is exact for the declared model.  The
second contains a discrete search over topologies and continuous estimation of branch lengths and
substitution parameters.  Its parameter space has the schematic form
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak M_R
=\coprod_{T\in\mathcal T_R}
\left(
\mathbb R_{>0}^{\,|E(T)|}\times\mathcal Q
\right),
\label{eq:phylogenetic-model-space}
\end{equation}
a union of topology-specific continuous regions whose boundaries meet when branches contract.
Ordinary gradient descent operates within one continuous region; a topology move, relaxation, or
sampling rule is needed to cross between them.

The number of unrooted binary topologies on $R$ labeled leaves is
\begin{equation}
N_R=(2R-5)!!,
\label{eq:number-unrooted-trees}
\end{equation}
so maximum-likelihood and Bayesian procedures must search or sample a vast outer space.  UPGMA,
neighbor joining, parsimony, local NNI or SPR moves, stochastic search, and tree-space MCMC are
different responses to this topology problem.  Modern implementations such as IQ-TREE 2 improve
search, substitution models, model selection, bootstrap approximations, and parallel evaluation
while retaining the inner likelihood recursion \cite{saitou1987,minh2020iqtree}.

Rooting supplies a further type distinction.  If the substitution process is reversible,
\begin{equation}
\pi(a)M_t(a,b)=\pi(b)M_t(b,a),
\label{eq:phylo-detailed-balance}
\end{equation}
the likelihood can be evaluated by placing the computational root anywhere on the same unrooted
tree.  The chosen root orients the dynamic program but is not thereby statistically identified.
A molecular clock, an outgroup, temporal samples, or a declared nonreversible process supplies
additional information for evolutionary direction.  Computational direction and biological time
direction must therefore be audited separately.

\BookSection{Three graphs occupy three different base spaces}

For an MSA $X\in[q]^{R\times L}$, three graph constructions must be kept separate:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
T_f &: \text{ancestral vertices and evolutionary branches},\\
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{pos}} &: \text{residue positions and typed categorical interactions},\\
A_i^{(\ell,h)}(X) &: \text{observed MSA rows and learned attention routes}.
\end{aligned}}
\label{eq:three-phylogeny-graphs}
\end{equation}
The first usually shares one topology across sites under the basic model, although recombination
or gene-tree discordance can require local trees.  The second acts within a sequence and may encode
structural or functional constraint.  The third is dense, layer- and input-dependent, generally
directed after normalization, and defined only on observed homologues.

The associated operators are also different.  A tree Laplacian acts on ancestral and observed
vertices.  The generator $Q$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:phylo-edge-kernel} acts on categorical states.  A
position Laplacian acts on residue positions after a position graph has been declared.  A
Kronecker-sum construction can combine such axes under special separability assumptions, but their
spectra are not interchangeable.  In particular, a leading singular vector across alignment rows
does not become a residue interaction mode merely because both are low rank.

An additive tree metric $d_{rs}$ satisfies the four-point condition: for every four leaves, the two
largest members of
\begin{equation}
d_{rs}+d_{uv},\qquad d_{ru}+d_{sv},\qquad d_{rv}+d_{su}
\label{eq:four-point-condition}
\end{equation}
are equal.  A generic attention-derived dissimilarity need not satisfy this condition, share a
topology across positions, compose along paths, or correspond to a Markov transition kernel.
Sequence similarity or clade-like clustering in column attention is therefore evidence of useful
row structure, not identification of a phylogenetic tree.

\BookSection{Static constraint and evolutionary dynamics are compatible}

Calling a Potts model static and phylogeny dynamic refers to two different coordinates.  A fitted
Potts Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
E_{\Theta_f}(x)
=-\sum_i h_i(x_i)-\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}(x_i,x_j)
\label{eq:phylo-potts-energy}
\end{equation}
is one family-level constraint field shared across MSA rows.  Evolution is a stochastic process
that transports sequences along the branches of $T_f$.  A hierarchical generative statement is
therefore
\begin{equation}
\Theta_f\sim P_\star(\Theta),
\qquad
(T_f,t_f)\sim P_{\mathrm{tree}},
\qquad
X_f\sim p(X\mid T_f,t_f,\Theta_f).
\label{eq:constraint-phylogeny-generative-model}
\end{equation}
The static field and the dynamic genealogy are not competitors: the first specifies which sequence
configurations are favored, while the second specifies how correlated observations were produced.

There is nevertheless a missing arrow in common practice.  A Gibbs distribution proportional to
$\exp[-E_{\Theta_f}(x)]$ is an equilibrium law, not an evolutionary transition kernel.  To evolve
complete sequences on a tree one must additionally specify rates
\begin{equation}
\mathcal Q_{\Theta_f}(x,x')
\quad\text{with, for example,}\quad
e^{-E_{\Theta_f}(x)}\mathcal Q_{\Theta_f}(x,x')
=e^{-E_{\Theta_f}(x')}\mathcal Q_{\Theta_f}(x',x),
\label{eq:potts-evolution-detailed-balance}
\end{equation}
if reversible evolution with the Potts equilibrium is intended.  This generator acts on a
$q^L$-state sequence space and is generally intractable.  Classical site-independent phylogenetics
regains tractability by factorizing the edge law across sites; a Potts model regains tractability by
describing an equilibrium or using pseudolikelihood rather than exact evolutionary histories.
Neither approximation contains the other.

This separation exposes the central identifiability problem.  Correlated residues can arise from
shared constraint, while correlated rows arise from shared ancestry; finite and taxonomically
biased sampling couples the two in the empirical MSA.  Sequence weighting changes an estimator but
does not identify the evolutionary process, and a high-scoring Potts block need not be free of
phylogenetic contribution \cite{obermayer2014,dietler2022}.

\BookSection{What modern phylogenetic learning actually replaces}

Recent learning methods can be classified by the coordinate they change rather than by the broad
label ``deep phylogenetics'':
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item \emph{Posterior representation.}  Variational Bayesian phylogenetic inference uses
subsplit Bayesian networks to represent distributions over topologies; normalizing flows enrich
the branch-length posterior.  The phylogenetic likelihood remains the target
\cite{zhang2018vbpi,zhang2020flow}.
\item \emph{Tree-space parameterization.}  GeoPhy embeds topology distributions into continuous
geometric coordinates, while Dodonaphy combines hyperbolic embeddings with a differentiable
soft-NJ decoder.  These methods make optimization differentiable without turning a generic dense
matrix into a tree \cite{mimori2023,macaulay2024}.
\item \emph{Combinatorial sampling.}  PhyloGFN trains a generative flow network to sample diverse
high-reward or posterior-weighted trees, replacing part of topology exploration rather than the
biological definition of likelihood \cite{zhou2023phylogfn}.
\item \emph{Likelihood-free amortization.}  Phyloformer learns evolutionary distance estimation
and reconstruction from simulated alignments.  It can be faster and can absorb richer simulated
dependencies, but its conclusions inherit the training simulator and distribution-shift problem
\cite{nesterenko2025phyloformer}.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the modern frontier is not one universal successor to neighbor joining or pruning.  It is a
collection of learned proposal distributions, posterior approximations, continuous relaxations,
and simulator-trained estimators attached to different stages of the classical pipeline.

\BookSection{MSA attention is an amortized mixed signal}

An MSA Transformer is trained to predict masked residues, not to recover $(T_f,Q,t_f)$.  Its column
attention may exploit close homologues, clades, subfamilies, gaps, or taxonomic redundancy whenever
these improve reconstruction.  Its tied row attention may simultaneously collect conservation and
coevolutionary evidence across the same rows.  Consequently the learned representation is more
accurately described as
\begin{equation}
H_f
=\mathcal A_\theta
\bigl(X_f;\ \text{constraint},\text{ancestry},\text{sampling},\text{gaps}\bigr),
\label{eq:msa-mixed-amortization}
\end{equation}
where the semicolon lists latent causes, not supplied labels.  Successful masked reconstruction
does not by itself identify their decomposition.

The useful comparison with classical phylogenetics is therefore not ``old tree versus modern
attention.''  Classical phylogenetics declares a tree and substitution law, then performs exact
latent-state marginalization conditional on them.  MSA Transformer learns a cross-family rule that
compresses several latent causes into hidden states and dynamic routes.  It is more flexible but
less identified.  An explicit hybrid would supply a tree-conditioned row operator and reserve a
learned residual route for dependence not explained by that tree:
\begin{equation}
H^{(\ell+1)}
=\Phi_\theta\!\left(
H^{(\ell)},
\mathcal K_{T_f,Q,t_f}H^{(\ell)},
A_{\theta,\mathrm{res}}^{(\ell)}(X_f)H^{(\ell)}
\right).
\label{eq:phylo-neural-hybrid}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:phylo-neural-hybrid} is an architectural hypothesis, not a uniquely identified
additive decomposition.  It becomes scientific only when the tree component and residual are
tested by interventions or matched nulls.

\BookSection{A phylogeny-aware operator audit}

A defensible audit should preserve the distinction in Eq.~\ref{eq:three-phylogeny-graphs}:
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item infer or sample trees with uncertainty rather than naming leading row principal components
``phylogeny'' from magnitude alone;
\item simulate an independent-site or declared epistatic process along the fitted tree while
matching depth, gaps, composition, and taxonomic sampling;
\item pass real and null MSAs through the identical operator-extraction pipeline and compare full
typed blocks before scalar compression;
\item evaluate leave-clade-out transfer and clade-preserving interventions, because arbitrary row
shuffling destroys both ancestry and the sampling structure one intends to control;
\item report sensitivity across plausible topologies, branch lengths, substitution models, and
product versus empirical background measures.
\end{enumerate}
The resulting null-relative quantity
\begin{equation}
\Delta G_{i\leftarrow j}
=G_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{real}}
-\E_{\widetilde X\sim Q_{T_f}}
G_{i\leftarrow j}(\widetilde X)
\label{eq:phylo-null-operator}
\end{equation}
asks which operator structure exceeds that generated by the declared genealogy.  It does not prove
physical contact or causal epistasis; those remain separate validation coordinates.  Its advantage
is narrower and more rigorous: common ancestry is represented as a generative row-graph rather
than removed by an unlabeled spectral heuristic.

\BookSection{MSA observations and interaction operators occupy different spaces}

The one-hot encoding of an alignment with $N$ rows belongs to an observation space with three axes,
\begin{equation}

\otimes\mathcal H_{\mathrm{pos}}

\label{eq:msa-three-axes}
\end{equation}

$\mathcal H^0=\bigoplus_i\mathcal H_i^0$ is the operator domain.  The sequence axis indexes
correlated samples; position and category index the object estimated from those samples.  A
phylogenetic mode across MSA rows, a graph eigenvector across residue positions, and the constant
categorical mode removed by $P_i$ therefore live in three different spaces.

Let $w_n\geq0$ with $\sum_nw_n=1$, and define the weighted site marginal
\begin{equation}
p_i(a)=\sum_{n=1}^Nw_n\mathbf1[X_i^{(n)}=a].
\label{eq:weighted-msa-marginal}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
Y_{nia}
=\sqrt{w_n}\,

\label{eq:whitened-msa}
\end{equation}

\BookSection{Two estimators in one coordinate system}

The weighted marginal in Eq.~\ref{eq:weighted-msa-marginal}, after declared smoothing, provides a
family reference.  A Potts fit estimates one $\widehat J_{ij}$ and hence one static block
$\widehat G_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm P}=P_i\widehat J_{ij}P_j^{\mathsf T}$ per direction
\cite{morcos2011,ekeberg2013,cocco2018}.  This estimate depends on the fitting objective,
regularization, sequence weights, and finite MSA sample.  Gauge projection addresses parameter
redundancy after those estimation choices have been made.

A pretrained pLM takes a single query sequence at inference and directly yields the conditional
logits needed for Eq.~\ref{eq:dynamic-response}.  It has amortized information across its training
distribution and can therefore produce a dynamic response without an inference-time MSA.  An MSA
remains useful for specifying the family references $p_i$, the family background
distribution $\mu_{ij}$, the static mean $\overline G_{i\leftarrow j}$, and uncertainty under finite family
sampling.  The two estimators answer different questions:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{MSA route:}&\quad
\{x^{(n)}\}_{n=1}^N\longrightarrow\widehat G_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm P},\\
\text{pLM route:}&\quad
x\longrightarrow G_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{pLM}}(x_{-ij}).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:two-estimators}
\end{equation}

Their comparison contains several estimands.  Let $G_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{P},*}$ be the block
of the population minimizer of the declared Potts fitting risk; let
$G_{i\leftarrow j}^{F,(2)}$ be the pair component of the true family conditional log score under
the same anchor reference; and let
$\overline G_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{pLM}}=
\E_{C\sim\mu_{ij}}G_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{pLM}}(C)$.  If
$\widehat{\overline G}^{\mathrm{pLM}}$ is a sampled response mean, then
\begin{align}
\widehat G_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm P}
-\widehat{\overline G}_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{pLM}}
={}&\underbrace{\widehat G_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm P}
-G_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{P},*}}_{\text{Potts estimation}}
+\underbrace{G_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{P},*}
-G_{i\leftarrow j}^{F,(2)}}_{\text{pairwise objective or misspecification}}\nonumber\\
&+\underbrace{G_{i\leftarrow j}^{F,(2)}
-\overline G_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{pLM}}}_{\text{pLM transfer and response gap}}
+\underbrace{\overline G_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{pLM}}
-\widehat{\overline G}_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{pLM}}}_{\text{response sampling}}.
\label{eq:estimand-decomposition}
\end{align}
Common reference-centered coordinates make all four terms comparable; their magnitudes remain
empirical.  In
particular, agreement between a pseudolikelihood Potts optimum and a pLM response mean is a
testable transfer statement of the framework.

\BookSection{Null-relative evidence}

Correlated sampling can bias covariance, inverse-coupling, and sector estimates
\cite{obermayer2014,dietler2022}.  Let $O^{\mathrm{real}}$ be an estimated operator statistic and
let $Q_{\mathrm{null}}$ preserve declared nuisance structure, such as one-site composition or a
phylogenetic tree, while removing the dependence under test.  The null-relative contrast is
\begin{equation}
\Delta O
=O^{\mathrm{real}}
-\E_{\widetilde X\sim Q_{\mathrm{null}}}O(\widetilde X).
\label{eq:null-contrast}
\end{equation}
Both terms must be produced by the same extraction pipeline and transported to the same reference
fibers before subtraction.  The contrast updates the evidence for an estimated interaction while
leaving the node fiber and Potts parameter-gauge class fixed.  Likewise, APC and leading-mode
deletion are useful position-level corrections only when the removed direction is supported as
nuisance by a declared null or downstream validation \cite{wang2024}.

The sequence, position, and categorical axes must be kept separate.  Phylogenetic residualization
on MSA rows and eigenvector deletion on the final scalar graph produce different results because
pair counting, inversion, projection, and norms have a noncommuting order.

For a covariance-like null operator $\Sigma_0$ that is positive definite after regularization, relative
spectral enrichment can be defined by
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{\mathrm{real}}u_k=\lambda_k \Sigma_0u_k.
\label{eq:generalized-null-spectrum}
\end{equation}
Large generalized eigenvalues identify directions enriched relative to the null scale.  Contact
and mechanism interpretations enter through downstream validation.

\BookSubsection{Directed routes and stationary geometry}

Graph spectral analysis begins only after the edge object and its compression have been declared.
This first branch concerns a route matrix whose rows already sum to one.  It asks where routing mass
settles after repeated application and whether the stationary flow between every pair is balanced.

thermodynamic equilibrium belong to other constructions.
For a nonnegative row-stochastic attention route $P$, the native object is directed.  Assume first
that $P$ is irreducible, let $\pi^{\mathsf T}P=\pi^{\mathsf T}$ be its positive stationary law,
and write $\Pi_\pi=\diag(\pi)$.  Here $P_{ij}$ uses the query-to-source convention $i\to j$;
the value message in Eq.~\ref{eq:attention} travels along the oppositely oriented edge $j\to i$.
The convention must remain fixed when cycles are interpreted.  The adjoint in the stationary inner
product is
\begin{equation}
P^*_{\pi}=\Pi_\pi^{-1}P^{\mathsf T}\Pi_\pi,
\qquad
P^{\mathrm{rec}}=\frac12(P+P^*_{\pi}),
\qquad
P^{\mathrm{circ}}=\frac12(P-P^*_{\pi}).
\label{eq:routing-reciprocal-circulation}
\end{equation}
Both $P$ and $P^*_{\pi}$ are row-stochastic.  The first term is reversible, while the second has
zero row sum and records stationary directed circulation.  A dimensionless detailed-balance defect
is
\begin{equation}
\delta_{\mathrm{db}}(P)
=\frac{\|\Pi_\pi P-P^{\mathsf T}\Pi_\pi\|_F}
{\|\Pi_\pi P\|_F}.
\label{eq:detailed-balance-defect}
\end{equation}
It vanishes exactly when $P=P^*_{\pi}$.  Equation~\ref{eq:softmax-detailed-balance} shows why the
criterion uses stationary flux: unequal stationary masses can make a reversible route entrywise
asymmetric.  Doubly stochastic normalization fixes $\pi$ to the uniform law while still allowing
asymmetric directed cycles, so bistochasticity and reversibility remain different constraints
\cite{sander2022,shahbazi2025}.

When both directional fluxes are positive, the same departure can be summarized by the entropy
production functional
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{EP}(P)
=\frac12\sum_{i,j}
(\pi_iP_{ij}-\pi_jP_{ji})
\log\frac{\pi_iP_{ij}}{\pi_jP_{ji}}\geq0.
\label{eq:routing-entropy-production}
\end{equation}
One-way support, as in a causal mask, requires a separate support diagnostic or an explicit
smoothing convention; otherwise Eq.~\ref{eq:routing-entropy-production} can diverge.  Reducible
routes likewise require restriction to recurrent classes or a declared regularization.  These are
properties of a computational Markov route.  A thermodynamic entropy-production interpretation
additionally requires an independently identified physical stochastic dynamics
\cite{schnakenberg1976}.  Context-conditioned Markov-chain and interacting-particle analyses
motivate studying route dynamics; output response and biological causation remain separate audits
\cite{ildiz2024,geshkovski2023}.

The directed random-walk Laplacian
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{rw}}=I-P
\label{eq:directed-random-walk-laplacian}
\end{equation}
is generally non-self-adjoint and can have nonorthogonal modes.  The reversible component admits
the symmetric representative
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{rec}}
=I-\Pi_\pi^{1/2}P^{\mathrm{rec}}\Pi_\pi^{-1/2},
\label{eq:reversible-laplacian}
\end{equation}
which is the additive reversibilization used in a directed-graph Laplacian \cite{chung2005},
whereas $P^{\mathrm{circ}}$ must be retained separately.  Left/right singular subspaces,
stationary fluxes, and nonnormality in the $\pi$-geometry retain directed transport omitted by an
ordinary eigenbasis.  For a frozen attention-only rollout across layers, the
route is the ordered product $P^{(L)}(x)\cdots P^{(1)}(x)$; the input-dependent factors form a
noncommuting product in general.  A spectrum of the
mean route, a mean of layer spectra, and a spectrum of the product are therefore different
statistics.

The practical output of this branch is therefore a three-part report: stationary mass $\pi$,
reversible routing $P^{\mathrm{rec}}$, and residual circulation $P^{\mathrm{circ}}$.  A single
symmetric spectrum sees only the second part.  A causal residue-to-residue response is tested by a
separate intervention on model outputs.

\BookSubsection{Scalar, signed, and connection Laplacians}

The next branch starts from interaction estimates.  Its Laplacian depends on what the signal
carried by a node is allowed to remember.  A scalar Laplacian retains only strength, a signed Laplacian also retains one declared
polarity, and a connection Laplacian retains a transported categorical direction.  Moving down this
list preserves more structure but also requires more modeling choices.

An arbitrary signed or block-valued response has a native operator geometry; a Markov law arises
only after an additional nonnegative normalization.  Its directionality should be audited through the block reciprocity split
in Eq.~\ref{eq:reciprocal-irreversible} and through typed left/right singular geometry before any
nonnegative scalar normalization is imposed.  Only if the scientific question deliberately
discards edge direction should one transport all directed blocks to common anchor fibers and
define a nonnegative symmetric static shadow, for example
\begin{equation}
A_{ij}^{\mathrm{stat}}
=\frac12\left(M_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathrm{static}}
+M_{j\leftarrow i}^{\mathrm{static}}\right),
\qquad A_{ii}^{\mathrm{stat}}=0.
\label{eq:static-adjacency}
\end{equation}
Assuming positive degrees, let
$D_{\mathrm{deg}}=\diag(A^{\mathrm{stat}}\mathbf1)$ and
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{sym}}^{\mathrm{stat}}
=I-D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{-1/2}A^{\mathrm{stat}}D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{-1/2}.
\label{eq:scalar-laplacian}
\end{equation}
Its quadratic form is
\begin{equation}
u^{\mathsf T}L_{\mathrm{sym}}^{\mathrm{stat}}u
=\frac12\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}^{\mathrm{stat}}
\left(\frac{u_i}{\sqrt{d_i}}-\frac{u_j}{\sqrt{d_j}}\right)^2.
\label{eq:scalar-dirichlet}
\end{equation}
Low eigenvectors are therefore position fields that vary slowly across strong static edges, and the
zero mode is $D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{1/2}\mathbf1$ \cite{shuman2013}.  This Laplacian is invariant under
any replacement of the full blocks that preserves their norms.  It can identify positional domains
or bottlenecks.  Signs, singular directions, substitution-specific coherence, and directed
circulation have already been removed by this compression.

A signed positional score requires a different construction.  If a declared categorical
contraction produces a symmetric $S_{ij}\in\R$, define
\begin{equation}
D_{|S|}=\diag\left(\sum_j|S_{ij}|\right),
\qquad L_{\pm}=D_{|S|}-S.
\label{eq:signed-laplacian}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
u^{\mathsf T}L_{\pm}u
=\frac12\sum_{i,j}|S_{ij}|
\left(u_i-\operatorname{sign}(S_{ij})u_j\right)^2\geq0.
\label{eq:signed-dirichlet}
\end{equation}
Positive edges favor aligned position signals and negative edges favor opposing signals
\cite{kunegis2010}.  The sign must come from a declared projection of the categorical operator or
from a signed routing variable.  A block norm has discarded that information.  A negative spectral
edge encodes opposition in the declared contraction; probability and biological antagonism have
their own meanings.

To retain categorical contrast orientation on an undirected support, work in the anchor-whitened fibers
$\widetilde{\mathcal H}_i^0=Q_i\R^q$ and choose for each undirected edge
an orthogonal transport $U_{i\leftarrow j}:\widetilde{\mathcal H}_j^0\to
\widetilde{\mathcal H}_i^0$ satisfying
$U_{j\leftarrow i}=U_{i\leftarrow j}^{\mathsf T}$.  The polar factor of
$\widetilde G_{i\leftarrow j}$ restricted to the centered fiber supplies one such orientation when
that restriction is nonsingular; a rank-deficient fiber map requires a declared completion or
restriction to its supported singular subspace.  For symmetric nonnegative
weights $a_{ij}=a_{ji}$, the connection energy is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conn}}(f)
=\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}
\left\|f_i-U_{i\leftarrow j}f_j\right\|_2^2
=\langle f,\mathbb L_{\mathrm{conn}}f\rangle.
\label{eq:connection-laplacian}
\end{equation}
The resulting connection Laplacian is positive semidefinite \cite{singer2012}.  Its low modes are
categorical contrast fields that remain coherent after edge-specific transport.  The scalar
Laplacian asks where a mode is smooth; the connection Laplacian additionally asks how a residue
contrast must rotate between positions.  Singular blocks admit several transport completions, so
the chosen completion is an audited modeling coordinate.

Thus the connection construction computes smoothness only after neighboring categorical vectors
have been aligned by the chosen transports.  It can reveal a coherent substitution pattern that a
norm graph erases.  Uniqueness, allosteric interpretation, and correspondence to microscopic motion
remain validation questions.

If $L=V\Lambda V^{\mathsf T}$ denotes either a scalar or connection Laplacian, the graph Fourier
coefficients of a compatible signal $y$ are $\widehat y_k=v_k^{\mathsf T}y$, and a spectral filter
is
\begin{equation}
g(L)y=Vg(\Lambda)V^{\mathsf T}y.
\label{eq:graph-fourier-filter}
\end{equation}
The heat kernel $e^{-tL}$ probes progressively coarser interaction neighborhoods as $t$ increases;
band-pass filters can separate domain-scale modes from localized edge variation.  Low frequency
means smoothness relative to the inferred graph; conservation, phylogeny, allostery, and function
enter through calibrated correspondences.

\BookSection{Routing geometry and persistent relational state}

Standard attention chooses a nonnegative row-stochastic route
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{ij}=\frac{e^{z_{ij}}}{\sum_k e^{z_{ik}}},
\qquad y_i=\sum_j\alpha_{ij}V_j.
\label{eq:simplex-routing}
\end{equation}
For fixed values, $y_i$ lies in $\operatorname{conv}\{V_j\}$.  A more general interaction operator
may use signed coefficients with bounded total variation.  If
$\sum_j|a_{ij}|\leq1$, then
\begin{equation}
y_i=\sum_ja_{ij}V_j
\in\operatorname{conv}(\{0\}\cup\{V_j,-V_j\}_j),
\label{eq:absolute-convex-routing}
\end{equation}
which permits source-specific contrasts and cancellation.  As one illustrative design, a
continuous signed sparse map is
\begin{equation}
w_{ij}=\operatorname{sign}(z_{ij})(|z_{ij}|-\lambda)_+,
\qquad
a_{ij}=\frac{w_{ij}}{\epsilon+\sum_k|w_{ik}|}.
\label{eq:signed-threshold-routing}
\end{equation}
It has exact zeros and bounded total variation, but superiority to softmax, sparsemax, entmax, or
other signed attention rules is an empirical question \cite{martins2016,peters2019,ye2024}.

Routing geometry is separate from edge-value capacity.  A node-only message-passing layer retains
$O(Ld)$ persistent states even if it evaluates dense transient connectivity.  A dense pair track
retains $O(L^2d_p)$ relational state, as in structure models with explicit pair representations
\cite{jumper2021}.  A degree-$k$ operator graph retains $O(Lkd_p)$ edge state once support is
known.  Categorical rank and interaction order are independent capacity coordinates.

State dynamics is another coordinate.  The same $\mathbb K_z$ may define a one-pass readout,
an iterated map, or part of a descent dynamics.  A Hopfield claim combines a specified state space,
fixed memories, update schedule, and Lyapunov decrease in addition to graph support.

\BookSection{Endpoint-removable context}

\begin{definition}[Endpoint-removable context]
For a deleted set $A\subseteq[L]$, a context map $C_{-A}:[q]^L\to\mathcal S$ is
endpoint-removable if $x_{-A}=x'_{-A}$ implies $C_{-A}(x)=C_{-A}(x')$.
\end{definition}

Endpoint-removable context must be constructed before information propagates from the deleted
positions; post hoc subtraction leaves their traces in retained states.  The following construction
establishes realizability for associative token-local recurrences as one concrete design.

\begin{theorem}[Associative affine deletion]
\label{thm:affine-deletion}
Let token $k$ define the affine transition
$F_k(h)=A_k(x_k)h+b_k(x_k)$ and augmented matrix
\begin{equation}
M_k(x_k)=
\begin{bmatrix}A_k(x_k)&b_k(x_k)\\0&1\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
For a deleted set $A$, replace $M_k$ by the identity when $k\in A$.  Then
\begin{equation}
M_{-A}=M_L^{(A)}M_{L-1}^{(A)}\cdots M_1^{(A)}
\label{eq:affine-deletion}
\end{equation}
is endpoint-removable and preserves the order of every retained transition.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every deleted factor is the identity and is independent of the deleted category.  All remaining
token-local factors occur in their original order, while associativity permits arbitrary
parenthesization.
\end{proof}

The theorem applies exactly to the affine recurrence core.  Surrounding convolutions,
normalizations, and context-dependent parameter generators require their own deletion laws.  A segment-product data structure can
evaluate a cavity with $|A|=m$ by composing at most $m+1$ retained intervals.

\BookSection{Strict targetwise conditional operators}

For a target $i$ and selected source set $N_i$, the shared strict context is the
whole-neighborhood cavity
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_i^E
=\sigma\{X_k:k\notin\{i\}\cup N_i\}.
\label{eq:whole-neighborhood-cavity}
\end{equation}
Every reference, route decision, coefficient, and pair table directed into $i$ must be measurable
with respect to this same cavity.  Only the final categorical lookup may observe a selected source
state $x_j$.

\begin{definition}[Strict targetwise support]
A directed router is strict for target $i$ if $N_i(x)=N$ implies $N_i(x')=N$ whenever $x'$ differs
from $x$ only on $\{i\}\cup N$.  For a randomized router the condition is pathwise after fixing a
seed independent of the sequence.
\end{definition}

Conditioned on $c_i^E$, define
\begin{align}
\widehat G_{i\leftarrow j}(c_i^E)
&=P_i(c_i^E)S_{i\leftarrow j}(c_i^E)P_j(c_i^E)^{\mathsf T},\\
\ell_i(a)
&=b_i(a;c_i^E)
+\sum_{j\in N_i}a_{i\leftarrow j}(c_i^E)
\widehat G_{i\leftarrow j}(a,x_j;c_i^E).
\label{eq:strict-conditional-operator}
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional pairwise operator]
\label{prop:strict-operator}
If $N_i$ is strict and every object in Eq.~\ref{eq:strict-conditional-operator} is measurable with
respect to $\mathcal C_i^E$, then the interaction branch is exactly pairwise in the active endpoint
variables under the declared product reference.  Its only access to a selected source category is
the final lookup $\widehat G_{i\leftarrow j}(a,x_j;c_i^E)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Condition on the cavity and the realized support.  All tables, references, and coefficients are
fixed while $(x_i,x_{N_i})$ vary.  Theorem~\ref{thm:pair-anova} centers each target--source term,
and additivity over $j$ excludes components involving two active sources.  Theorem
\ref{thm:pair-truncation} identifies the branch as the pairwise truncation.
\end{proof}

Projection with an ordinary full-context table remains a useful local gauge.  The stricter global
functional semantics comes from the shared cavity construction above.

\BookSection{Dynamic support is directed and targetwise}

Let a deterministic router $E:[q]^L\to\mathcal G_L$ output an undirected graph.  Call it globally
endpoint-invariant if changing the categories of any active vertices leaves the entire realized
graph unchanged.

\begin{theorem}[Spanning-support no-go]
\label{thm:global-support-no-go}
If a globally endpoint-invariant router has one input for which its output graph has no isolated
vertices, then the router is constant on $[q]^L$.  Equivalently, every output of a nonconstant
globally endpoint-invariant router contains at least one isolated vertex.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a realized graph with no isolated vertices, every sequence position is active.  The complement
of the active set is empty, so every other input agrees outside the active set.  Global endpoint
invariance therefore forces the same graph for every input.
\end{proof}

Targetwise routing remains adaptive under the theorem.  $N_i(x)$ stays fixed when its own target
and realized sources vary, while other residues may still alter routes for targets to which they
are unselected.  The resulting predictive object is a directed cavity operator; an undirected
contact graph requires a separate identification step.

\BookSection{Single-sequence recoverability and optional-MSA supervision}

Let $T_{ij}\in\{0,1\}$ be a training-only support label,
$Y_{ij}=X_{[L]\setminus\{i,j\}}$, and
$Z_{ij}(X)=\mathbf1\{j\in N_i(X)\}$ for a strict router.  Strictness implies that $Z_{ij}$ is a
function of $Y_{ij}$ alone.  Indeed, invariance to $x_i$ is immediate.  If $j$ is selected,
invariance to $x_j$ follows from the definition.  If $j$ is not selected but changing $x_j$ made it
selected, applying strictness at the modified input while reverting $x_j$ would force the modified
support at the original input, a contradiction.  Define
\begin{equation}
e_{ij}^{\mathrm{cav}}
=\E\min\{\eta_{ij}(Y_{ij}),1-\eta_{ij}(Y_{ij})\},
\qquad
\eta_{ij}(y)=\Pr(T_{ij}=1\mid Y_{ij}=y).
\label{eq:cavity-bayes-floor}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Cavity Bayes floor]
\label{prop:cavity-bayes-floor}
Every strict deterministic or independently randomized router satisfies
\begin{equation}
\Pr(Z_{ij}\ne T_{ij})\geq e_{ij}^{\mathrm{cav}}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conditional on $Y_{ij}=y$, endpoint invariance restricts the prediction to a label that observes
neither endpoint.  The minimum conditional zero-one risk is the smaller posterior mass.
Independent randomization has the same or larger linear conditional risk.  Taking expectation
proves the claim.
\end{proof}

Motif replacement, coherent local-window controls, and homolog exclusion then test whether the
output response is tied to a proposed computational mechanism \cite{zhang2024motifs}.

\item \emph{Architectural and biological intervention.}  Report support size, categorical rank,
higher-order residual, and persistent state memory separately.  Endpoint interventions must leave
context, support, references, coefficients, and raw pair tables invariant for every strict claim.
Compare strict cavity, local-gauge, dense-attention, and pair-track baselines; report routing
calibration, captured teacher mass, set recall, and the gap to the cavity Bayes floor.  A Hopfield
claim additionally requires Lyapunov decrease and fixed-point tests.  Finally, compare static,
dynamic, and higher-order operators with matched structural, double-mutant, and multi-background
experimental endpoints.
\end{enumerate}

These tests distinguish an operator framework from an architectural proposal.  The theory can
fail informatively: a pLM mean may not agree with family Potts fields; dynamic variance may be
dominated by off-manifold substitutions; reciprocity may fail; or pairwise components may leave a
large higher-order residual.  Contact prediction from a response tensor supports reusable
evolutionary statistics, but not by itself a discrete internal motif library, a physical energy,
or a causal biological interaction.

The scalar graph and the full operator make different predictions.  If
$F_{ij}(a,b)$ is a measured log-fitness or quantitative phenotype for a double-mutant panel around
reference residues $(a_0,b_0)$, define experimental epistasis
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{ij}(a,b)
=F_{ij}(a,b)-F_{ij}(a,b_0)-F_{ij}(a_0,b)+F_{ij}(a_0,b_0).
\label{eq:experimental-epistasis}

The operator-level comparison is between $G_{ij}(a,b)$ and the corresponding projected epistasis
table in a common reference, rather than only between $M_{ij}$ and a binary contact label

Model logits, evolutionary energies, and measured fitness generally occupy different units and may
be related nonlinearly.  Their comparison therefore uses a scale or link function fitted on a
training subset and evaluated on held-out mutations, rather than direct numerical equality.

The unifying object is the typed interaction system in Eq.~\ref{eq:typed-system}, with the block
operator $\mathbb K_z$ as its
pairwise carrier.  Statistical models, latent-variable models, message-passing networks, and
spectral estimators become comparable after their fibers, fields, support, edge map, context,
order, state dynamics, normalization, and estimation law have been declared.  Models that
coincide in one coordinate may differ in all the others.

The Potts--pLM bridge illustrates this distinction.  Potts learns one family-shared additive rule
from related sequences.  A Transformer learns shared parameters that map a new sequence background
to a conditional computation.  Its parameters are fixed after training, but the response graph is
input dependent.  Proposition~\ref{prop:exact-recovery} identifies the additive limit,
Theorem~\ref{thm:static-dynamic} separates persistent and contextual response, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:dynamic-higher-order} identifies the dynamic variance with higher-order
functional mass under a product reference.  Proposition~\ref{prop:reciprocity} supplies the
additional condition required before directed responses can be integrated into a joint Potts
energy.

The RBM--Hopfield--attention comparison occupies different coordinates.  A Bernoulli RBM permits
multiple latent motifs and induces interactions of all orders after marginalization.  A classical
Hopfield--Potts factorization is a low-rank static pair energy.  Modern Hopfield retrieval is a
categorical competition among memories with a Lyapunov function under stated conditions.  One
attention readout can share its algebra, while a Transformer stack changes keys, values, memories,
and state across layers.  The common log-sum-exp primitive establishes a local algebraic
correspondence; the complete probabilistic and dynamical systems remain distinct.

The framework also separates function geometry from probability geometry and graph spectral
geometry.  Weighted projection identifies the bivariate tangent component.  Matrix scaling maps a
score to one exact marginal-preserving pair law, while global compatibility of all such pairs is a
stronger marginal-polytope problem.  A scalar norm produces a position Laplacian, a signed
contraction produces a signed Laplacian, and declared transport between categorical fibers produces
a connection Laplacian.  Their low modes encode three different notions of coherence.  Spectral
filtering acts downstream of interaction identification, so its resolution is bounded by the
information retained when the graph was built.  APC, inverse covariance, heat kernels, and
leading-mode operations are therefore useful transformations of declared objects, each with a
specific statistical meaning.

MSA and pLM inference are complementary estimators of a common operator coordinate.  A pLM supplies
an amortized response without an inference-time MSA.  An MSA supplies a
family-specific empirical distribution, reference geometry, recurring substitutions, and finite-
sample uncertainty.  Agreement between an MSA-derived static field and the pLM response mean is a
testable transfer claim.  Sequence weighting and phylogenetic residualization alter the estimator
while the definition of categorical interaction stays fixed.

Architectural consequences follow after semantics are fixed.  At the computational level, a
Transformer is a dynamic GNN; at the interaction level, the identified operator comes from an
audited categorical response.  Dense transient routes, persistent pair tracks, sparse signed
routing, and endpoint-removable cavities make different memory and measurability tradeoffs.  The
support no-go and cavity Bayes floor quantify the limits that strict conditional meaning places on
content-dependent routing and single-sequence recoverability.  Optional MSA supervision changes
the training signal while those inference-time information constraints remain.

Six limitations remain structural.  First, reference transport preserves the gauge class but not
the numerical norm, so the anchor measure remains part of every static average and ranking.
Second, the exact identification of dynamic variance with higher-order mass requires a product
reference that may be far off the natural sequence manifold; correlated empirical backgrounds are
closer to that manifold but introduce nonorthogonal cross terms.  The two quantities and their
geometry gap in Eq.~\ref{eq:dynamic-geometry-gap} must therefore be reported separately.  Third, a Laplacian
spectrum depends on scalarization, sign convention, transport, normalization, and null graph;
inside a nearly degenerate band only the eigenspace is stable.  Fourth, a row-stochastic route has
no unique undirected spectrum until a stationary law, support convention, and symmetrization are
declared; causal masks can make the route reducible or one-way.  Fifth, the Potts population
optimum, true family pair component, and pLM response mean remain distinct estimands as shown in
Eq.~\ref{eq:estimand-decomposition}.  Sixth, symmetry or reversibility of an attention route does
not establish reciprocity of the end-to-end response, much less biological cause.  Contacts,
allosteric networks, phylogeny, and experimental epistasis remain conclusions of matched nulls and
interventions.

Most individual mathematical ingredients---functional ANOVA, matrix scaling, Gaussian covariance
selection, spectral geometry, and Bayes risk---are classical.  The contribution is their typed
assembly around one interaction system, the reference-transport and exact gauge-factorization
links, and the explicit boundaries between Potts parameter redundancy, dynamic response, latent
memory,
state dynamics, statistical estimation, and biological meaning.

\BookChapter{Conclusion}

Protein sequence models can be placed in one mathematical framework without being declared the
same model.  The common carrier consists of fields and a block operator on the direct sum of
reference-centered feature fibers.  Weighted projection selects a canonical representative of a
Potts parameter-gauge class, reference transport compares representatives, and Sinkhorn scaling
supplies an
exact pair law on the same quotient.  Support and routing determine where an operator is executed;
context determines whether it has conditional meaning; functional ANOVA determines its
interaction order; an update rule determines whether it is a readout or a dynamical system; and
MSA or pLM inference determines how it is estimated.  Scalar, signed, and connection Laplacians
then provide distinct multiscale spectra on the position graph or the full categorical bundle;
they are downstream transforms of the identified operator, not alternative definitions of it.

Within this framework, Potts couplings are static reciprocal edge operators and pLM substitution
responses are generally context-dependent effective operators.  Hopfield patterns and RBM motifs
are different latent factorizations.  Transformer attention is dynamic graph computation, while
only a frozen single-layer value path is restricted to the span of its head maps; the complete
categorical response must be audited at the output.  Probability-exact pair laws, scalar position
shadows, spectral nuisance removal, and biological validation are successive transformations and
claims, not synonyms for the interaction itself.  Stationary-measure reversibility further
separates row-normalization asymmetry from genuine directed circulation: a symmetric logit kernel
can yield an entrywise asymmetric but reversible route, whereas a doubly stochastic route can
still be nonreciprocal.  The framework therefore converts informal
unification into mathematical coordinates, transport rules, compatibility conditions, estimand
gaps, and falsifiable experiments.

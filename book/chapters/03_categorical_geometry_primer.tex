\section{A finite-dimensional categorical geometry primer}
\label{sec:book-categorical-geometry-primer}

The preceding table calculation uses ordinary finite-dimensional linear algebra.  A vector
$f\in\mathbb R^q$ assigns one numerical score to each residue category.  A reference law $p$
assigns weights to those scores: $p^{\mathsf T}f$ is their mean and
$\sum_a p(a)f(a)^2$ is their squared norm.  The notation $L^2(p)$ records this weighted geometry.
Removing the mean leaves $q-1$ independent contrasts.

Three constructions below organize these contrasts.  A \emph{fiber} is the contrast space at
one position.  A \emph{direct sum} collects one such vector per position.  A \emph{tensor product}
represents joint contrasts across several positions.  Whitening changes weighted coordinates into
Euclidean ones, where singular vectors identify the source and target directions of an edge map.

The quotient language also has a concrete meaning.  Adding a target-only term, a source-only term,
or a constant to a pair table leaves its bivariate component unchanged.  All tables related by
these additions form one equivalence class.  A chosen reference selects its centered
representative.  The definitions and formulas below make these operations explicit.

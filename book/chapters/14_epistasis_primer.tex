\section{A four-genotype grammar for experimental interaction}
\label{sec:book-epistasis-primer}

Consider reference residues $(a_0,b_0)$ at sites $(i,j)$, an alternative $a$ at site $i$, and an
alternative $b$ at site $j$.  A linked double-mutant experiment measures all four genotypes in the
same molecular construct and assay pipeline:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}c|cc@{}}
 & $j=b_0$ & $j=b$ \\
\hline
$i=a_0$ & $Y_e(a_0,b_0;c)$ & $Y_e(a_0,b;c)$ \\
$i=a$   & $Y_e(a,b_0;c)$   & $Y_e(a,b;c)$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here $Y_e(\cdot;c)\in\mathcal Y_e$ is the endpoint of assay $e$ under condition and sequence
background $c$.  Choose a declared scalar coordinate $g_e:\mathcal Y_e\to\mathbb R$ and define
$F_e=g_e\circ Y_e$.  Experimental epistasis in that coordinate is the mixed finite difference
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{ij}^{(e,g)}(a,b;c)
=F_e(a,b;c)-F_e(a,b_0;c)-F_e(a_0,b;c)+F_e(a_0,b_0;c)
=\Delta_i\Delta_jF_e.
\label{eq:book-epistasis-primer}
\end{equation}
The additive null in the $F_e$ coordinate has $\epsilon_{ij}^{(e,g)}=0$.  A complete substitution panel repeats
the four-genotype comparison across many choices of $a$ and $b$, producing a categorical table
rather than one scalar pair label.

The coordinate is part of the null.  For a positive scalar endpoint $Y_e$, the identity choice
$g_e(y)=y$ tests additive endpoint effects.  The logarithmic choice $g_e(y)=\log y$ gives
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{ij}^{(e,\log)}(a,b;c)
=\log\frac{Y_e(a,b;c)Y_e(a_0,b_0;c)}
{Y_e(a,b_0;c)Y_e(a_0,b;c)},
\end{equation}
so zero epistasis tests a multiplicative endpoint model.  A nonlinear transformation can change the
numerical epistasis and occasionally its sign.  The assay, endpoint, units, transformation, reference
genotype, condition, and uncertainty must accompany the table.  If a competition assay directly
identifies the Malthusian rate $m_c$, choosing $F_e=m_c$ yields Malthusian-growth epistasis with a
population-dynamic interpretation.  Other assay coordinates retain their own experimental meaning.

The remaining residues form a sequence background $C$.  Repeating the panel in several backgrounds
defines $\epsilon_{ij}^{(e,g)}(a,b;C,c)$ and tests whether the interaction is stable or modulated by other
sites.  Assay conditions supply another background: temperature, binding partner, expression
system, time point, and cellular environment can all change the measured relation.  Compensation
means that the double perturbation restores the chosen endpoint relative to the declared
single-mutant null; it does not identify a physical contact by itself.

Replicates, matched controls, and an explicit treatment of missing or low-count variants are needed
to separate biological nonadditivity from measurement noise.  A scalar structural contact can test
the endpoints of a positional prediction.  A reference-aligned epistasis table retains the
substitution-specific degrees of freedom needed to test a categorical edge operator, with a fitted
link function when model scores and experimental measurements use different scales.

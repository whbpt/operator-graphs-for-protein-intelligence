\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Coda: spectra inherit every earlier choice}

Scalar modes describe positional smoothness; signed modes retain a chosen polarity; connection
modes compare transported categorical directions; directed decompositions retain circulation.
Gauge projection, scalarization, symmetrization, normalization, and context averaging determine
which of these objects is available.  Near a degenerate band, the subspace projector is the
stable comparison.

A physical mode needs a corresponding operator: vibration requires stiffness and a mass metric,
while relaxation requires a dynamical generator.  In particular, categorical connection modes
act on amino-acid contrasts, not atomic displacements.  Their correspondence with sectors or
allosteric motion must be tested against the relevant observations.

The next part asks whether a trainable architecture can realize the declared interaction under
its information and execution constraints.

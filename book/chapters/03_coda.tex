\section*{Coda: the graph after gauge}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Coda: the graph after gauge}

A declared reference selects one centered representative of a pair-table equivalence class and
turns it into a map between categorical contrast spaces.  In a compatible Potts Hamiltonian,
adding $u(a)+v(b)+c$ merely reallocates terms among the pair, the one-body fields, and the energy
zero.  The quotient removes this freedom.  Scalar contact scores are a later compression.

Gauge projection and endpoint invariance remain separate: centering a table does not control the
information used to generate it.  The next chapter therefore asks what happens when one residue
is varied while another prediction is observed.  A Potts model gives the same pair response in
every background; a nonlinear sequence model can give a field of responses.

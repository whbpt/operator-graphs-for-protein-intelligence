\section{One probability flow, two path laws}
\label{sec:book-diffusion-transport}

AF3 replaces the AF2 structure module with a diffusion model over all-atom coordinates.  This
section isolates the mathematical object introduced by this replacement and the scope of its
physical interpretation.  Let $R_0$ denote a clean structure and $R_t$ a noised version.  A
denoiser or score-like network is trained under a forward corruption process and conditioned on the
trunk representations.  Here $(s,z)$ denotes the fixed single and pair conditioning state
introduced in Chapter~\ref{chap:book-alphafold}.  In score notation, the learned field is associated with
\begin{equation}
s_\theta(R_t,t\mid s,z)
\approx
\nabla_{R_t}\log p_t(R_t\mid s,z).
\label{eq:book-af3-score}
\end{equation}
This probability space consists of noisy coordinate configurations conditional on the model inputs
and trunk state.  Attention, distograms, and masked-token heads normalize over three other sample
spaces: source positions, distance bins, and amino-acid identities.

The compact formula contains three different objects: a deliberately chosen corruption process, a
vector field estimated from corrupted data, and a numerical transport procedure.  Their separation
locates what diffusion contributes.

\subsection{The forward SDE smooths information}

A continuous-time idealization of Gaussian corruption is the It\^o stochastic differential
equation (SDE)
\begin{equation}
\mathrm dR_t=f(R_t,t)\,\mathrm dt+g(t)\,\mathrm dW_t,
\qquad 0\leq t\leq T,
\label{eq:book-af3-forward-sde}
\end{equation}
where $f$ is a prescribed drift, $g$ is a noise amplitude, and $W_t$ is Brownian motion.  An SDE
specifies infinitesimal transition laws for an ensemble of nonsmooth paths: over a short interval
$\Delta t$, the deterministic displacement is of order $\Delta t$, while the random displacement
is of order $\sqrt{\Delta t}$.  Its ensemble density obeys the Fokker--Planck equation
\begin{equation}
\partial_t p_t(R)
=-\nabla_R\!\cdot\!\bigl[f(R,t)p_t(R)\bigr]
+\frac12g(t)^2\Delta_Rp_t(R).
\label{eq:book-af3-fokker-planck}
\end{equation}
The first term transports probability; the second smooths it.  In the drift-free case this is the
heat equation.  If the added variance is $\sigma_t^2$, then
\begin{equation}
p_t=p_0*\mathcal N(0,\sigma_t^2I)
=\exp\!\left(\frac{\sigma_t^2}{2}\Delta_R\right)p_0.
\label{eq:book-af3-heat-semigroup}
\end{equation}
Thus forward diffusion is Gaussian convolution, or heat-semigroup smoothing, viewed at a sequence
of resolutions.  Fine features of the data density disappear first; at sufficiently large noise,
the terminal law is chosen to be close to a simple reference distribution.

The common finite-time parameterization
\begin{equation}
R_t=\alpha_tR_0+\sigma_t\epsilon,
\qquad \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I),
\label{eq:book-af3-gaussian-corruption}
\end{equation}
is the transition law of an appropriate linear SDE.  The forward schedule is a design choice, and
its clock records noise scale.  A physical-time interpretation requires an independently identified
molecular dynamics.

\subsection{Denoising estimates the score of each smoothed density}

At a fixed noise level, the score is the spatial log-density gradient
\begin{equation}
s_t(R\mid s,z)=\nabla_R\log p_t(R\mid s,z).
\label{eq:book-af3-conditional-score}
\end{equation}
It points toward locally increasing probability density and contains no normalization constant.
For the Gaussian corruption in Eq.~\ref{eq:book-af3-gaussian-corruption}, least-squares noise
prediction estimates a conditional mean,
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_\theta(R_t,t,s,z)
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E[\epsilon\mid R_t,s,z],
\qquad
s_t(R_t\mid s,z)
=-\frac{1}{\sigma_t}\mathbb E[\epsilon\mid R_t,s,z].
\label{eq:book-af3-noise-score}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, a minimum-square denoiser estimates the posterior mean of the clean coordinates.
Tweedie's identity gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[R_0\mid R_t,s,z]
=\frac{R_t+\sigma_t^2s_t(R_t\mid s,z)}{\alpha_t}.
\label{eq:book-af3-tweedie}
\end{equation}
Noise prediction, clean-coordinate denoising, and score estimation are different parameterizations
of the same conditional object when the Gaussian kernel and scaling convention are fixed.  The
essential learned quantity is a multiscale family of density-gradient fields from which clean
structures are generated.

The posterior over the particular $\epsilon$ used for one corrupted example retains uncertainty
given $R_t$.  Squared-error training returns its conditional expectation, which is precisely the
quantity needed to reconstruct the score of the noised population.

\subsection{The reverse SDE transports noise toward data}

For the forward process in Eq.~\ref{eq:book-af3-forward-sde}, the time-reversed diffusion has drift
\begin{equation}
\mathrm dR_t=
\left[f(R_t,t)-g(t)^2\nabla_R\log p_t(R_t\mid s,z)\right]\mathrm dt
+g(t)\,\mathrm d\overline W_t,
\qquad t:T\longrightarrow0,
\label{eq:book-af3-reverse-sde}
\end{equation}
where $\overline W_t$ is Brownian motion in the reverse-time filtration
\cite{anderson1982,song2021}.  The displayed differential is integrated with decreasing $t$; this
convention matters when interpreting its signs.  The score correction turns a generic noisy
reference law back toward the learned conditional data law.  Replacing the unknown score by
$s_\theta$ and discretizing the equation yields a sampler.

The reverse SDE is a probability-generating dynamics.  Its individual paths are algorithmic
transport paths; Brownian increments are introduced by the model, the drift depends on a
noise-indexed training density, and time runs backward along an artificial corruption schedule.
An exact score recovers the intended data distribution.  Molecular kinetics additionally requires
a physically calibrated forward-time process.

\subsection{The probability-flow ODE exposes the deterministic core}

The same forward marginals $p_t$ also satisfy the continuity equation generated by the ordinary
differential equation (ODE)
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm dR_t}{\mathrm dt}
=f(R_t,t)-\frac12g(t)^2\nabla_R\log p_t(R_t\mid s,z).
\label{eq:book-af3-probability-flow-ode}
\end{equation}
Indeed,
\begin{align}
-\nabla_R\!\cdot\!\left[
\left(f-\frac12g^2\nabla_R\log p_t\right)p_t
\right]
&=-\nabla_R\!\cdot(fp_t)+\frac12g^2\Delta_Rp_t,
\label{eq:book-af3-ode-continuity}
\end{align}
which is exactly Eq.~\ref{eq:book-af3-fokker-planck}.  Run from the terminal reference law at $T$
back to time zero, this probability-flow ODE transports the same one-time marginal distributions
as the reverse SDE when the score is exact \cite{song2021}.

An ODE assigns a smooth deterministic path once its initial point is fixed.  An SDE continues to
inject Brownian randomness along the path.  Their one-time marginal densities agree, while their
path measures, couplings between times, numerical errors, and exploration behavior remain different.
In the usual sampling experiment the ODE still begins from a random terminal point drawn from the
reference law.  The shared mathematical core is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\text{multiscale density smoothing}
\longrightarrow
\text{conditional score estimation}
\longrightarrow
\text{reverse probability transport}.}
\label{eq:book-af3-diffusion-core-appendix}
\end{equation}
SDE and ODE samplers are two realizations of the last arrow.  With an approximate neural score and
a finite step size, each incurs a characteristic law-approximation error; sampler choice is a
numerical and statistical decision.

Diffusion changes the epistemic status of the output.  Multiple coordinate samples can be drawn
from the same conditioning state, allowing the generator to express some multimodality and sampling
variability.  Calibration of sample diversity additionally depends on the learned score, the
terminal law, the numerical solver, and any selection or ranking imposed after generation.

\subsection{Statistical energy and molecular energy}

The score is the gradient of a learned log density.  A physical equilibrium force additionally
requires a calibrated density with physical units and temperature, an appropriate measure on
configuration space, correct treatment of solvent and chemical state, and a relation such as
\begin{equation}
F(R)=-\nabla_R U(R)
\end{equation}
for the intended physical potential.  The diffusion score follows the noised training distribution
and depends on the conditioning network.  Its native interpretation is a generative vector field;
molecular force-field or kinetic interpretations enter through the additional physical structure.

One may always define a noise-dependent statistical energy
\begin{equation}
E_t^{\mathrm{stat}}(R\mid s,z)=-\log p_t(R\mid s,z)+C_t,
\qquad
s_t=-\nabla_RE_t^{\mathrm{stat}}.
\label{eq:book-af3-statistical-energy}
\end{equation}
To identify $k_{\mathrm B}T\,s_0$ with a thermodynamic mean force requires the learned density to
equal the relevant equilibrium distribution under the correct configuration-space measure and
experimental conditions.  A kinetic interpretation further requires a physically valid mobility,
noise law, and forward-time dynamics.

Repeated diffusion samples reflect the learned structural distribution, training data,
conditioning information, noise schedule, and sampling procedure.  Comparing sample frequencies
with experimental populations requires separate calibration.

\section{Three solvable lenses on interaction}

Three lines of statistical mechanics are especially useful for the distinctions developed in this
book: Bernard Derrida's random-energy landscapes, Marc M\'ezard's cavity inference, and Haim
Sompolinsky's dynamical response theory.  They expose three different operations on a
many-variable system.

The three cases also organize three common translation failures.  A global energy law need not have
a useful low-order graph.  A message on an edge is derived from a probability model but is not its
pair potential.  A fixed parameter matrix can produce a response operator that changes with state
and time.  Each case will reappear where its native mathematics becomes useful: random energy in
the interaction-order chapter, dynamical mean field in the static--dynamic chapter, and cavity
equations in the probability chapter.

This placement follows the objects rather than the chronology.  It turns a historical lineage
from equilibrium statistical mechanics through nonequilibrium dynamics, optimization,
neuroscience, and artificial intelligence into a sequence of exact audits.  Protein-facing
claims enter only after the corresponding state space, operator, estimator, and validation
endpoint have been declared.

The three lenses distinguish operations on a many-variable system.  Protein science adds a second
issue: one claim may also cross sequence, conformational, functional, evolutionary, and
observational scales.  The next section makes those maps explicit before the operator formalism
begins.

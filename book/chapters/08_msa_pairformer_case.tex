\section{Case study: MSA Pairformer and the paired-alignment estimand}
\sectionmark{MSA Pairformer and the paired-alignment problem}

MSA Pairformer supplies a compact empirical case for the progression developed in this chapter.
It keeps an alignment state $m_{si}$, maintains a persistent pair state $z_{ij}$, writes from the
alignment into the pair track through an outer product, and updates pair state through triangular
paths.  The model has 111 million parameters and was trained by masked-token prediction on
269,455 single-chain UniClust30 alignments.  Its protein--protein interaction behavior appears when
a paired alignment is supplied at inference time, even though multichain alignments and complex
structures were absent from pretraining \cite{akiyama2026msapairformer}.

The case contains two statistical experiments.  Single-chain alignments support masked-token and
intrachain contact evaluations.  Protein--protein interaction evaluations first construct a paired
alignment, thereby declaring which homologue of chain $A$ and which homologue of chain $B$ form one
joint observation.  Parameter count describes the learned maps in the middle of these pipelines;
it does not specify the information in the input sample or the endpoint used to score the output.

The mathematical audit will therefore ask five questions in order.  What empirical measure over
MSA rows is induced by the query?  What statistic of that measure is written into pair state?  How
do row-space transport and triangular path composition close the recurrent update?  Which output
law is identified by query-only masking, and which internal factors remain nonidentifiable?  For a
protein--protein interaction, what joint sample is created by the pairing rule?  The reported
111M-versus-15B comparison can then be interpreted as a consequence of the full estimator rather
than as its starting explanation.

The finite estimator and the claims it can support come first.  Rank bounds, continuum limits,
residual Jacobians, assignment relaxations, and the full adaptive-route audit follow in
Section~\ref{sec:book-msa-pairformer-math}.

\subsection{The mathematical object: a measure--edge dynamical system}

\subsubsection{The coupled state and its three contractions}

Each MSA Pairformer layer performs three coupled operations.  Pair-weighted averaging lets the
pair track shape communication along each sequence.  A query-biased outer product writes
alignment evidence back into the pair track.  Triangle multiplicative updates refine $(i,j)$ by
paths through third residues $k$.  Schematically,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
m^{(\ell+1/3)}_{si}
&=\mathsf{PWA}_{\theta}\!\left(m^{(\ell)},z^{(\ell)}\right)_{si},\\
z^{(\ell+2/3)}_{ij}
&=z^{(\ell)}_{ij}
+W_{\theta}\!\left[
\sum_s w_s(q;m^{(\ell+1/3)})
a(m_{si})\otimes b(m_{sj})
\right],\\
z^{(\ell+1)}_{ij}
&=z^{(\ell+2/3)}_{ij}
+\sum_k\Phi_{\theta}\!\left(z_{ik}^{(\ell+2/3)},z_{jk}^{(\ell+2/3)}\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:book-msa-pairformer-layer}
\end{equation}
The sequence weights $w_s$ depend on the query.  Two queries embedded in the same broad family can
therefore induce different effective family samples.  The pair track stores the resulting
relational evidence, while triangle paths give the model a direct computational route for
combining two-hop paths.  The equation is still only a schematic until the normalization spaces
and the missing-data denominator are made explicit.  That algebra is the mathematical core of the
model.

\subsubsection{What the outer-product write estimates}

Let $M\in\mathbb R^{S\times L\times d_m}$ denote one encoded MSA, with $s$ indexing sampled
homologues and $i$ indexing aligned residue columns.  These axes have different meanings: $s$ is
an empirical-sample axis, while $(i,j)$ is the edge index of a complete residue-position graph.
For two learned maps $A,B:\mathbb R^{d_m}\to\mathbb R^{c}$, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
u_{si}&=A(\operatorname{LN}M_{si}),qquad
v_{sj}=B(\operatorname{LN}M_{sj}),\\[-2pt]
\mathcal O_{q,ij}
&=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_s\omega_s(q)\,\chi_{si}\chi_{sj}
        \,u_{si}\otimes v_{sj}}
       {d_{q,ij}},\\[-2pt]
d_{q,ij}&=\sum_s\omega_s(q)\chi_{si}\chi_{sj},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:msa-pairformer-exact-cross-moment}
\end{equation}
where $\chi_{si}$ is the observed-token mask.  The released implementation computes this
quantity by multiplying both factors by $\sqrt{\omega_s}$ and dividing by $d_{q,ij}$ after the
contraction.  With complete alignments $d_{q,ij}=1$; with gaps, every residue pair has its own
conditional empirical measure
\begin{equation}
\mu_{q,ij}
=\frac1{d_{q,ij}}\sum_s\omega_s(q)\chi_{si}\chi_{sj}\,\delta_s,
\qquad
\mathcal O_{q,ij}=\mathbb E_{\mu_{q,ij}}[u_{si}\otimes v_{sj}].
\label{eq:msa-pairformer-pairwise-measure}
\end{equation}
Thus the outer product is a weighted cross-moment.  It becomes a covariance only after centering
$u$ and $v$ under the same pair-specific measure.  A shared linear map and a small hidden width
compress this moment before a SwiGLU and a projection write it into $z_{ij}$.

The query update is an exponential tilt of an empirical MSA measure.  Its exact covariance identity
and the distinction between feature, router, and denominator terms are collected in
Section~\ref{sec:book-msa-pairformer-math}.
\subsubsection{Alternating contractions close two index spaces}

The MSA update has the complementary contraction
\begin{equation}
\beta^h_{ij}=w_z^h{}^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{LN}(z_{ij}),qquad
A^h_{ij}=\operatorname{softmax}_{j}(\beta^h_{ij}),qquad
\Delta m_{si}=W_o\operatorname{concat}_{h}\left[
g^h_{si}\odot\sum_jA^h_{ij}v^h_{sj}\right],
\label{eq:msa-pairformer-pwa-operator}
\end{equation}
with $v^h_{sj}$ and $g^h_{si}$ obtained from normalized MSA features.  The pair-to-MSA map is a
row-wise transport on the position graph; the MSA-to-pair map in Eq.~\ref{eq:msa-pairformer-exact-cross-moment}
contracts the sample axis and expands a position edge.  The model's basic algebra is this
alternation of three index operations: contraction over homologues $s$, expansion to $(i,j)$,
and path composition over mediators $k$.

\subsubsection{The closed operator and its asymptotic limits}

The finite model is an ordered nonlinear composition of pair-to-MSA transport, query-weighted
MSA-to-pair writing, and triangle updates.  Its typed domains, continuum limits, rank bounds,
parameterisation gauge, and large-$S$ limits are collected in
Section~\ref{sec:book-msa-pairformer-math}.  The pairing rule enters through the observed rows and
the query enters through the empirical row measure, so replacing the sequence-to-edge lift by a fixed
family average changes the operator.
\subsection{Adaptive row measure and identification}

\subsubsection{Query bias as a maximum-entropy row measure}

The released model uses two pre-softmax query--key fields.  Suppressing layer and batch indices,
\begin{equation}
r_s(q;M)
=\frac1{\sqrt d}\frac1{L_s}\sum_i\chi_{si}
\left(q_{1i}^{\mathsf T}k_{1si}
-\lambda\,q_{2i}^{\mathsf T}k_{2si}\right),
\qquad
\omega_s=\frac{\rho_s e^{r_s}}{\sum_t\rho_t e^{r_t}},
\label{eq:msa-pairformer-differential-row-score}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda=\exp(\langle\lambda_{q1},\lambda_{k1}\rangle)-
\exp(\langle\lambda_{q2},\lambda_{k2}\rangle)+\lambda_0^{(\ell)}$, where $\rho_s$ is the row-validity
mask and $L_s=\sum_i\chi_{si}$.  In the released depth-22 model, using zero-based layer index
$\ell$, $\lambda_0^{(\ell)}=0.8-0.6\exp(-0.3\ell)$.  The score is averaged over the columns
present in row $s$ and the final softmax is over valid MSA rows.  This is the precise mathematical
novelty often hidden by the phrase ``query-biased attention.''
\subsubsection{What query-only masking identifies}

Let $I$ be a random set of query positions and let $M_{0,-I}$ denote the visible query residues.
The second training stage optimizes the denoising risk
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{query}}
=\mathbb E_{M,I}\left[-\sum_{i\in I}
\log p_\theta\!\left(M_{0i}\mid M_{0,-I},M_{1:S}\right)\right].
\label{eq:msa-pairformer-query-pseudolikelihood}
\end{equation}
This is a query-conditional pseudolikelihood/denoising objective.  It does not define or maximize
a joint Potts likelihood unless additional compatibility conditions are imposed on all conditional
logits.  Randomly changing the query row trains a row-exchangeable rule: a permutation of the
non-query rows leaves the weighted moment unchanged up to the same permutation of weights.

Under a mixture model with latent subfamily $H_s$, a Bayes-optimal relevance score would contain a
log density ratio such as $\log p(M_s\mid q,H_s)/p(M_s\mid q)$, but the paper does not prove that
the learned score has this form.  The defensible statement is therefore query-conditioned
empirical reweighting, with posterior interpretation treated as a hypothesis to test.

\subsubsection{Symmetry and identifiability of the row axis}

Row permutation equivariance, query exchange, and the distinction between paired and unpaired chain
permutations are recorded in Section~\ref{sec:book-msa-pairformer-math}.  The main consequence is
that changing the query or the partner assignment changes the empirical reference law.
\subsubsection{Three risks, three estimands}

The two language-model stages optimize different conditional risks.  With a random mask set
$J_s$ on every row, the first stage is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{all}}
=\mathbb E_{M,J}\left[-\sum_{s=0}^{S-1}\sum_{i\in J_s}
\log p_\theta(M_{si}\mid M_{-J})\right],
\label{eq:msa-pairformer-all-row-risk}
\end{equation}
whereas query-only masking is Eq.~\ref{eq:msa-pairformer-query-pseudolikelihood}.  The latter
changes the information available to the target row and trains the network to use the visible
homologues as context.  The infinite-capacity population optimum identifies the conditional output
law; it does not identify a unique decomposition into $q,k$, $\omega$, pair features, and triangle
messages.

The contact benchmark adds a separate supervised risk for a frozen representation,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{contact}}(\eta)
=-\sum_{(i,j)}\left[y_{ij}\log c_{ij}^{\eta}
+(1-y_{ij})\log(1-c_{ij}^{\eta})\right],
\label{eq:msa-pairformer-contact-probe-risk}
\end{equation}
with $c_{ij}^{\eta}$ read from a selected pair layer.  MP-EM and MP-PDP then define a third,
assignment-dependent objective through Potts pseudolikelihood or its differentiable relaxation.
Consequently, a contact score can improve while the masked-token risk is unchanged, and a pairing
score can improve through the MRF head without proving that the base language model has learned a
general partner posterior.

\subsection{Pair-state path algebra and residual depth}

\subsubsection{Triangle multiplication is a length-two path closure}

The triangle block has a precise tensor contraction.  After input normalization and learned gates,
let $a_{ik},b_{ik}\in\mathbb R^{c}$ be the two projected versions of the pair edge.  The outgoing
and incoming updates are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf T_{\mathrm{out}}(z)_{ij}
&=\sum_k a_{ik}\odot b_{jk},\\
\mathsf T_{\mathrm{in}}(z)_{ij}
&=\sum_k a_{ki}\odot b_{kj}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:msa-pairformer-triangle-contractions}
\end{equation}
If projections, gates, and normalization are suppressed and the pair state is scalar, these become
$ZZ^{\mathsf T}$ and $Z^{\mathsf T}Z$.  Each output edge therefore aggregates all length-two
walks $i\!\to\!k\!\to\!j$ or $k\!\to\!i, k\!\to\!j$ in a shared feature space.  Under a factor
model $z_{ik}\approx r_i^{\mathsf T}h_k$ and $z_{jk}\approx r_j^{\mathsf T}h_k$, the contraction
measures overlap of the two endpoint--mediator factor sets.  This gives a path-consistency
interpretation for the operation.

The interpretation has a strict boundary.  A triangle contraction is generally neither an inverse
covariance nor a direct-coupling estimator: it need not be positive semidefinite, symmetric,
energy-decreasing, or gauge-fixed.  The ablation in the paper supports a task-specific benefit of
the path channel; it does not identify the channel with $C^{-1}$ or prove that every selected false
positive is an indirect statistical correlation.  In the language of cavity methods, the operation
is a representation-level surrogate for combining a mediator.  It does not perform cavity deletion,
variable marginalization, or normalization over a probabilistic factor graph.

\subsubsection{Residual depth is a nonlinear transport process}

Residual depth transports perturbations through a coupled MSA--pair state.  The full transport
Jacobian, singular-value diagnostics, and independent scalar readouts are collected in
Section~\ref{sec:book-msa-pairformer-math}; residual form alone carries no Lyapunov or free-energy
interpretation.
\subsection{Architectural lineage: why it looks like AlphaFold}

The resemblance is architectural and direct.  MSA Pairformer builds on the MSA module of
AlphaFold 3, whose operations descend from the MSA--pair organization of the AlphaFold 2
Evoformer \cite{jumper2021,abramson2024}.  All three systems treat a residue pair as a persistent
state variable, write alignment statistics into that state, and refine it through triangular
paths.  MSA Transformer keeps the MSA tensor as its principal state and exposes position routes
through axial attention; it has no corresponding persistent $L\times L\times d_z$ pair track.

The four computational records can be placed side by side:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{MSA Transformer:}&\quad
M^{(0)}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M^{(D)}
\longrightarrow \text{token logits or attention contact readout},\\
\text{AlphaFold 2:}&\quad
(M^{(0)},Z^{(0)})\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow(M^{(D)},Z^{(D)})
\longrightarrow \text{equivariant structure module},\\
\text{AlphaFold 3:}&\quad
(M,Z)\longrightarrow (s,Z)
\longrightarrow \text{conditional all-atom diffusion},\\
\text{MSA Pairformer:}&\quad
(M^{(0)},Z^{(0)})\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow(M^{(D)},Z^{(D)})
\longrightarrow \text{masked-token logits}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:book-msa-pairformer-alphafold-lineage}
\end{equation}
AlphaFold 2 preserves bidirectional MSA--pair communication through the Evoformer and then maps
the result into coordinates.  AlphaFold 3 uses a shorter MSA module to deposit evolutionary
evidence into pair state, discards the explicit MSA rows, and lets its Pairformer and diffusion
modules complete structural generation.  MSA Pairformer repeatedly applies the MSA-module style
of computation and reads the result back into amino-acid probabilities.  Its scientific role is
therefore a family-conditioned language model, even though its relational state and update algebra
come from structure-prediction architectures.

The main architectural modification is the query-biased write from $M$ into $Z$.  A standard
outer-product mean uses a uniform row average,
\begin{equation}
O_{ij}^{\mathrm{uniform}}
=\frac1R\sum_{s=1}^{R}a(M_{si})\otimes b(M_{sj}),
\label{eq:book-uniform-outer-product-mean}
\end{equation}
whereas MSA Pairformer uses
\begin{equation}
O_{ij}^{q}
=\sum_{s=1}^{R}w_s(q)\,
a(M_{si})\otimes b(M_{sj}).
\label{eq:book-query-biased-outer-product-lineage}
\end{equation}
This changes the AlphaFold-style relational trunk from a global family aggregator into a
query-conditioned estimator.  The training contract makes that change identifiable.  Pretraining
used uniform sequence weights and masked reconstruction throughout the MSA.  During the second
stage, homologous rows remained visible, only the designated query row was masked, and a different
sufficiently covered row could become the query at each epoch.  The row weights were therefore
trained to estimate relevance to the current query rather than reproduce one global family
average.  The training endpoint is conditional token reconstruction.  The structural inductive bias remains
present in the choice of pair state and triangle paths, while masked-token prediction supplies the
learning signal.

A relational computation developed for structure prediction has therefore been isolated from
coordinate generation and trained to estimate evolutionary conditionals.  Whether that transferred
inductive bias improves a particular endpoint is assessed only after the paired sample and readout
have been declared.

\subsection{Paired MSAs define the protein--protein interaction sample}

\subsubsection{What joint sample does the pairing rule create?}

Suppose two interacting protein families provide separate alignments
\begin{equation}
\mathcal M_A=\{a_u\}_{u\in U},
\qquad
\mathcal M_B=\{b_v\}_{v\in V}.
\label{eq:book-two-unpaired-msas}
\end{equation}
These collections do not yet define cross-chain covariance.  Within organism or genomic context
$g$, there may be several paralogues $U_g\subset U$ and $V_g\subset V$.  A pairing rule chooses a
partial assignment
\begin{equation}
\widehat\pi_g:U_g\longrightarrow V_g
\label{eq:book-paired-msa-assignment}
\end{equation}
and constructs concatenated rows
\begin{equation}
\mathcal M_{AB}^{\widehat\pi}
=\left\{a_u\Vert b_{\widehat\pi_g(u)}:
u\in U_g,\ g\in\mathcal G\right\}.
\label{eq:book-paired-msa-construction}
\end{equation}
Only Eq.~\ref{eq:book-paired-msa-construction} turns residues in the two chains into jointly
observed variables.  Different assignments preserve the single-chain alignments while changing
the cross-chain joint table.  For positions $i$ in $A$ and $j$ in $B$, a weighted covariance block
has the form
\begin{equation}
\widehat C_{ij}^{\widehat\pi}(x,y)
=\sum_u w_u
\mathbf 1[a_{u,i}=x]
\mathbf 1[b_{\widehat\pi_g(u),j}=y]
-\widehat p_i^A(x)\widehat p_j^B(y).
\label{eq:book-cross-chain-covariance-pairing}
\end{equation}
The marginals in the second term can remain unchanged while the first term moves from a strong
coevolutionary pattern to noise.  Pairing error can erase a real interface signal, and a pairing
rule correlated with taxonomy or genomic organization can introduce structure that the downstream
model will exploit.

The assignment $\Pi=(\pi_g)_g$ is therefore a latent combinatorial variable.  The attenuation and
total-variance formulas are collected in Section~\ref{sec:book-msa-pairformer-math}.
\subsubsection{Query bias changes the empirical reference law}

The query-biased outer product produces one normalized weight for each concatenated MSA row.  For
query $q$ and pairing $\widehat\pi$, it defines the empirical measure
\begin{equation}
\widehat\mu_{q,\widehat\pi}
=\sum_u w_u(q;\mathcal M_{AB}^{\widehat\pi})
\delta_{a_u\Vert b_{\widehat\pi_g(u)}}.
\label{eq:book-query-pairing-empirical-law}
\end{equation}
This is an adaptive reference law over observed evolutionary backgrounds.  It changes with the
query and with the pairing rule.  Calling the weights ``attention'' describes the operation;
Eq.~\ref{eq:book-query-pairing-empirical-law} states their statistical effect.  In the presence of
gaps, the exact pairwise law is the conditional, pair-specific measure in
Eq.~\ref{eq:msa-pairformer-pairwise-measure}, rather than one global row distribution.

The response-regulator experiment makes that effect visible.  OmpR sequences formed only about
$12\%$ of a 4,096-sequence mixed-family alignment.  Uniform averaging recovered one of 19
subfamily-specific interface contacts, whereas query bias recovered eight.  Some strongly weighted
sequences had low raw sequence identity to the query.  The estimator had learned a relevance rule
richer than nearest-neighbor selection.

The benefit is conditional on family heterogeneity.  When a property is shared globally or every
row supplies useful evidence, query weighting can reduce effective alignment depth and need not
improve the estimate.  The broader estimator also depends on a deep, trustworthy alignment:
evolutionarily recent and de novo proteins, disordered or distantly alignable regions, and sequence
rearrangements that violate one-to-one column correspondence remain weakly supported regimes.

This adaptivity also creates a dependency that an operator audit must retain:
\begin{equation}
G_{i\leftarrow j}
=G_{i\leftarrow j}
\left(C;\mathcal M_A,\mathcal M_B,\widehat\pi,
\widehat\mu_{q,\widehat\pi}\right).
\label{eq:book-pairformer-conditioned-operator}
\end{equation}
A reference-centered categorical Jacobian extracted from the model is closest to the edge operator
developed in this book.  The hidden $z_{ij}$ is a computational edge state, and the scalar contact
head is a readout from that state.  Neither object alone identifies the directed categorical
response in Eq.~\ref{eq:book-pairformer-conditioned-operator}.

\subsubsection{The externally comparable object is a finite categorical response}

Fix an MSA, a pairing, a masked target $i$, and an audit source $j$.  For source residue $b$ and
target logit category $a$, define
\begin{equation}
T^{\mathcal M}_{i\leftarrow j}(a,b)
=\ell_i\!\left(a\mid M_{0j}=b,M_{0i}=\mathrm{mask},\mathcal M_{-0}\right),
\qquad
G^{\mathcal M}_{i\leftarrow j}=P_iT^{\mathcal M}_{i\leftarrow j}P_j^{\mathsf T}.
\label{eq:msa-pairformer-finite-categorical-response}
\end{equation}
This finite table, after reference centering, lives in the same categorical operator space as the
Potts and pLM responses developed earlier in the book.  It includes every path from the perturbed
residue through the MSA state, adaptive row weights, pair state, triangle blocks, and language-model
head.
\subsection{Task-conditional efficiency after the estimand is fixed}

On 45 CASP15 monomer targets shorter than 1,024 residues, MSA Pairformer achieved median long-range
contact precision $P@L=0.66$, compared with $0.50$ for both MSA Transformer and ESM2-15B.  Its 111M
parameters are approximately $0.74\%$ of 15B.  The comparison licenses a task-specific claim:
explicit family evidence, persistent pair state, and triangle computation can dominate parameter
scale for long-range contact extraction.  ProteinGym variant-effect performance remained strong,
with an average Spearman correlation of $0.45$ over 217 substitution datasets.  The result does not
establish a universal ordering across generation, remote homology, structure prediction, binding
affinity, or low-homology proteins.

The contact benchmark also contains a small supervised readout.  The pretrained representation was
frozen and a logistic contact head was fitted on the same 20 trRosetta proteins used in prior MSA
Transformer and ESM evaluations.  ``Unsupervised contact prediction'' describes the language-model
objective; the end-to-end contact score still contains labeled structural calibration.

The efficiency result is therefore a statement about an estimator-resource pair.  The model
receives a structured family sample, compresses it through a finite bilinear feature bottleneck,
retains a persistent relational workspace, and uses path closure before a task-specific readout.
The 111M-versus-15B contrast follows from that complete statistical and computational pipeline.

\subsection{What the experiments identify}

The paper supplies several useful interventions, each with a different scope.

\paragraph{Interface contacts.}
On 27 bacterial complexes that fit the MSA Transformer length limit, median interface $P@K$ was
$0.52$ for MSA Pairformer and $0.19$ for MSA Transformer.  Among 519 predictions with reported
probability above $0.75$, $83\%$ were within 8 Angstrom and $94\%$ within 12 Angstrom in the
experimental structure.  These results evaluate the complete proximity-pairing, representation,
and contact-readout pipeline.

\paragraph{Partner removal.}
In the ParD3--ParE3 toxin--antitoxin landscape, 9,194 antitoxin variants altered four interface
positions.  MSA Pairformer attained precision $0.74$ when ranking the 465 designated binders.
Removing the toxin sequences from the paired MSA reduced precision to $0.50$.  This intervention
shows that cross-chain rows carry information used by the score; it does not isolate whether the
gain comes from query weighting, pair state, triangle updates, or another learned component.

\paragraph{Pair assignment.}
For MALG--MALK, the reported pairing precision was $0.70$ and $0.67$ for the two MSA Pairformer
procedures, compared with $0.31$ for MSA Transformer DiffPALM.  This is evidence that the learned
family estimator can help infer partners.  Evaluation on two interactions leaves the general
paralogue-pairing problem open.

\paragraph{Triangle paths.}
In a separate supplemental ablation, replacing triangle updates by parameter-matched pair updates
reduced CASP15 long-range contact precision by roughly 16--18 percentage points, depending on the
training comparison.  About $41\%$ of the ablated model's selected false positives could be linked
to a third residue contacting both endpoints.  The experiment supports the hypothesis that
triangle paths provide a useful consistency channel and can suppress some mediated false positives
on this task.  Equation~\ref{eq:msa-pairformer-triangle-contractions} shows why the channel is
sensitive to common mediators; it does not establish an algorithmic equivalence with inverse
covariance or direct coupling analysis.

\subsection{A paired-MSA audit}

A protein--protein interaction claim should report the paired alignment as part of the estimator,
not as an incidental preprocessing file.  A minimal audit contains:
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item the sequence databases, search dates, coverage and identity thresholds for both families;
\item the taxonomic units, paralogue counts, genomic-neighborhood rule, and assignment constraints;
\item paired depth, effective depth, discarded candidates, fusion handling, and uncertainty over
ties;
\item a within-taxon random-pairing null, which preserves species composition and single-chain
marginals while destroying the proposed partner assignment;
\item a cross-taxon random-pairing null, which tests how much broader taxonomic structure drives the
score;
\item a partner-removal ablation and a depth-matched unpaired or single-chain baseline;
\item sensitivity of full categorical Jacobian blocks, query weights, and contact readouts to the
pairing rule;
\item separate reporting for bacterial operon-linked, eukaryotic paralogue-rich, and cross-species
systems.
\end{enumerate}

The strongest factorial experiment crosses pairing and architecture.  Run the same model on
curated, within-taxon-shuffled, cross-taxon-shuffled, and partner-removed alignments; then run a
Potts model, MSA Transformer, and MSA Pairformer on those same inputs.  The pairing contrast
estimates the value of the joint sample.  The model contrast estimates how well each method
extracts information from that sample.  Their interaction asks whether one architecture is
especially robust to pairing noise.

MSA Pairformer therefore connects the three levels of this book in one pipeline.  Mathematics
specifies a maximum-entropy row tilt, a pair-specific weighted cross-moment, a path-closure
dynamics, a latent permutation problem, and a finite categorical response operator.  Physics
supplies testable hypotheses about when compensatory substitutions and path consistency reflect an
interface geometry; no free energy is implied by the architecture alone.  AI supplies an amortized
estimator that converts a current family sample into persistent relational state.  This returns to
the observation principle of
Chapter~\ref{chap:book-observations}: the pairing rule is part of the estimator.  The next chapter
takes spectra only after this provenance has been recorded.

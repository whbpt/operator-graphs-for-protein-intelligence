\section[RFD4-Proteina: relational state]{RFD4-Proteina: writing, refining, and reading relational state}
\sectionmark{RFD4-Proteina: relational state}
\label{sec:book-rfd4-case}

RFD4-Proteina writes single states multiplicatively into pair memory, uses that memory to bias
token routing, refines both tracks repeatedly within one generation step, and delivers its
conditions through declared gauges \cite{corley2026rfd4proteina}.  The operator audit isolates
four objects: the centered component introduced by a write, the input response of repeated
refinement, the gauge content of a delivered condition, and the commitment of a readout along the
generation orbit.

\subsection{Declare the represented objects}

Each supported polymer residue has an anchor $x_i\in\mathbb R^3$ and a latent
$\ell_i\in\mathbb R^{32}$.  The autoencoder reconstructs identity, relative atom coordinates,
and atom presence; other components can remain explicit atom tokens.  Its self-attention mixes
tokens without a persistent pair track, while the flow trunk maintains single states $s_i$ and
pair states $z_{ij}$ (Supplementary Sections S3.1--S3.2).

A per-token latent is therefore not an endpoint-local encoding: changing one source may change
several latents.  Its width 32 bounds the differential rank of a path passing exclusively through
that latent, while anchors, other latents, and pair states supply additional paths.  The
categorical rank bound in Eq.~\ref{eq:etr-rank-bound} applies only to a specified intervention
and readout.

\subsection{Why multiplicative writes survive a pair audit}

In the flow trunk, normalized single states are projected and written into the pair track as
\begin{equation}
u_i=\operatorname{LayerNorm}(s_i),\qquad
a_i=W_au_i,\qquad b_j=W_bu_j,\qquad
z_{ij}\leftarrow z_{ij}+a_i\odot b_j.
\label{eq:rfd4-hadamard-write}
\end{equation}
Each channel lets one endpoint gate the other's contribution.  The reference-centered geometry
gives a precise comparison with additive writing.

\begin{proposition}[Centered additive and multiplicative writes]
\label{prop:rfd4-centered-write}
Fix an endpoint-excluding background, positive references $p_i,p_j$, and feature tables
$A\in\mathbb R^{q_i\times d_p}$ and $B\in\mathbb R^{q_j\times d_p}$ that depend only on
their respective endpoint categories.  Let $w\in\mathbb R^{d_p}$ be a fixed linear channel
readout.  The additive and multiplicative write tables are
\begin{align}
T^+&=(Aw)\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}+\mathbf1(Bw)^{\mathsf T},\\
T^\times&=A\operatorname{diag}(w)B^{\mathsf T}.
\end{align}
Their centered components satisfy
\begin{align}
P_iT^+P_j^{\mathsf T}&=0,\\
P_iT^\times P_j^{\mathsf T}
&=(P_iA)\operatorname{diag}(w)(P_jB)^{\mathsf T},
\label{eq:rfd4-centered-product}\\
\operatorname{rank}(P_iT^\times P_j^{\mathsf T})
&\leq\min\{d_p,q_i-1,q_j-1\}.
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $P_i\mathbf1=P_j\mathbf1=0$, both endpoint-only summands vanish.  Matrix multiplication
gives Eq.~\ref{eq:rfd4-centered-product}, and the rank bound follows from the ranks of its
factors.
\end{proof}

One product channel supplies an outer product of centered category profiles.  It vanishes if
either profile is constant or its readout weight is zero; dependence and cancellation can reduce
the rank across channels.  In the actual trunk, context-mixed $s_i$ may already depend on both
endpoints, so an additive write can inherit an interaction and later nonlinearities can create
one.  A multiplicative component can also disappear at readout.  The proposition isolates the
write under endpoint-local features and a fixed linear readout; it does not predict the complete
model's ablation performance.

\subsection{From pair memory through routes to output response}

RFD4 projects normalized pair states into head-specific biases:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{ij}^{h}
=\operatorname{softmax}_{j}\!\left(
\frac{(q_i^h)^{\mathsf T}k_j^h}{\sqrt{d_h}}+\beta_{ij}^{h}\right),
\qquad
\beta_{ij}^{h}=b_h\!\left(\operatorname{Norm}(z_{ij})\right).
\label{eq:rfd4-pair-route}
\end{equation}
Thus pair memory affects token routing, and routed token states subsequently write back into pair
memory.  Outgoing and incoming triangle multiplication further update a pair through pairs sharing
a token, schematically
\begin{equation}
\Delta z_{ij}
\sim\sum_k A(z_{ik})\odot B(z_{jk}).
\label{eq:rfd4-triangle-path}
\end{equation}
The third index gives a relational path; its statistical order requires the audit of
Chapter~\ref{chap:book-order-rank}.  All single, pair, coordinate, and latent paths enter
$\mathbb J^{\mathrm{lat}}$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:global-etr-factorization}, followed by the encoder
and readout maps.  Freezing $\alpha_{ij}^h$ keeps only value transport.  The full response also
includes the query, key, pair-bias, gate, and upstream derivatives of
Eq.~\ref{eq:attention-full-differential}.

The output contract needs equal care.  The flow trunk predicts coordinate and latent velocities,
with auxiliary heads for sequence identity and motif correspondence.  The auxiliary heads are
trained on detached single states, but their outputs still depend on those states at inference.
Sequence predictions also condition the decoder.  Consequently, an audit must include this
conditioning path when it is used.  Argmax choices, sampling, and Hungarian motif assignment
are discrete operations: local derivatives describe a fixed smooth branch, while finite
interventions can also change the selected branch.

RFD4 does not automatically supply the masked conditional required by
Eq.~\ref{eq:conditional-logit-table}.  To extract such a categorical object, an investigator must
define the target-withholding and source-substitution protocol.  The protocol must remove target
identity from every input intended to exclude it, including cached latents, side-chain geometry,
and atom-presence patterns.  Re-encoding the intervened structure and changing one latent while
holding the others fixed are different experiments.

\subsection{Conditions arrive through a gauge}

Motif and target geometry enter either as absolute coordinates or as internal coordinates,
produced by centering and independently rotating each coordinate group; noise added to
unconditioned groups carries independent per-group offsets; and noise aligned to stereochemical
frames fixes the corresponding angular gauge.  Propositions~\ref{prop:randomized-gauge}
and~\ref{prop:interpolant-statistic} type these choices.

Absolute conditioning transmits the deposited frame with the geometry; the frame is an arbitrary
gauge element, and the output placement is constrained by it without gaining information.  The
randomized internal-coordinate representative carries exactly the invariant geometry, so
placement must arrive through other inputs or the prior, and the conditional support over
placements is the orbit rather than a point.  Replacing a mandatory origin token by optional,
noisily specified chain centroids is the same declaration on the translational gauge.  Zero-mean
noise would expose the clean placement of every unconditioned group as an interpolant identity;
the per-group offsets close the identity, and applying them only outside coordinate-conditioned
regions withholds the offset from the condition.  The reported exchange of absolute for internal
target conditioning is consistent with this typing in direction; the propositions fix no
magnitude.

The gauge protocol joins the masking protocol of the previous subsection.  An intervention on a
condition must declare whether its frame is transmitted or redrawn; replicates over redrawn
gauges vary input realizations while holding invariant content fixed; and leakage checks on
cached conditions must include frame statistics.

\subsection{Recycling has repeated input injection}

The model refines hidden states repeatedly while holding the current noisy coordinates, latents,
and molecular conditions fixed.  This places two clocks in the computation: generation time and
within-step recurrent depth.  Hidden single and pair states are reset at each generation step;
within that step, each pass adds projected previous states to the original embeddings.

Write $u$ for the fixed-step inputs, including noisy coordinates, latents, times, and conditions;
let $e(u)$ be the initial single/pair embeddings and $m^{(r)}$ their recurrent state.  A schematic
form of the reported recurrence is
\begin{equation}
m^{(0)}=0,\qquad
m^{(r+1)}=F_\theta\!\left(e(u)+\mathcal P(m^{(r)});c_r(u)\right),
\qquad 0\leq r<R,
\label{eq:rfd4-recycle}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal P$ includes normalization and projection, and $c_r$ includes pass-dependent
conditioning.  Hold the discrete pass count $R$ fixed for the differential audit.  Along one
orbit define
\begin{equation}
A_r=D_1F_\theta\,D\mathcal P(m^{(r)}),\qquad
B_r=D_1F_\theta\,De(u)+D_2F_\theta\,Dc_r(u),
\label{eq:rfd4-recycle-factors}
\end{equation}
with both derivatives of $F_\theta$ evaluated at the arguments in
Eq.~\ref{eq:rfd4-recycle}.  The complete input sensitivity obeys
\begin{align}
Z_0&=0,\qquad Z_{r+1}=A_rZ_r+B_r,\\
Z_R&=\sum_{k=0}^{R-1}A_{R-1}\cdots A_{k+1}B_k,
\label{eq:rfd4-forced-response}
\end{align}
where the product is the identity for $k=R-1$.  For a smooth head $o=g(m^{(R)})$, its input
response is $Do=Dg(m^{(R)})Z_R$.

The sum is essential: every pass reinjects the original evidence.  The homogeneous product
$A_{R-1}\cdots A_0$ instead describes propagation of a hidden-state perturbation with inputs
fixed.  Even a stable homogeneous product can coexist with a substantial forced response from
the $B_k$ terms.  Parameter sharing also does not make the $A_r$ identical, because they are
evaluated at different states.  More passes can change response directions and path lengths
without adding a new set of trunk parameters; improvement, convergence, and Lyapunov descent
each require additional evidence.

The reported training recipe propagates gradients through only the final trunk pass.  That
optimization convention must be separated from the derivative of the executed inference
function.  An inference response audit must differentiate through, or numerically perturb,
all executed passes rather than inherit the training-time detach operations.

\subsection{A third clock: readout commitment}

Generation time and within-step depth are now separate clocks.  Temporal search reads a third:
the progress at which a readout of an intermediate state predicts the same readout of the final
state.  Fix a sampling procedure with declared shared randomness and trajectory states
$X^{(s)}$, $0\le s\le 1$.  For a readout $f$, define its commitment by
\begin{equation}
\kappa_f(s)
=1-\frac{\E\bigl[\operatorname{Var}\bigl(f(X^{(1)})\mid X^{(s)}\bigr)\bigr]}
{\operatorname{Var}\bigl(f(X^{(1)})\bigr)}.
\label{eq:rfd4-commitment}
\end{equation}

Commitment is an estimand, not a local object: it is not a derivative along the orbit and not a
function of the forced response in Eq.~\ref{eq:rfd4-forced-response}.  No monotonicity in $s$ is
guaranteed, and the ordering of commitment across readouts is an empirical property of the
trained field.  Measurement requires protocol declarations: how intermediates are evaluated
(single-step clean prediction or completed rollout), which randomness is shared (seeds and branch
decisions), the full conditioning path through sequence-conditioned decoding, and the treatment
of discrete branch selections.  Rank correlations between intermediate and final readouts, as
reported for interface area, hydrogen bonding, and predictor confidence, estimate a
commitment-type quantity under exactly such a protocol.

Checkpoint termination is a stopping decision measurable in the trajectory filtration; it
changes the selected ensemble through a selection layer and leaves the field untouched.  Both
time-varying recycle schedules and time-varying search rewards allocate against this clock.  The
observed resolution order---global interface properties before local contacts before predictor
confidence---is evidence about the trained field, not a consequence of the architecture.

\subsection{Probability transport, energy supervision, and selection}

Flow matching generates coordinates and residue latents jointly, with temporal score rescaling
and stochastic noise injection in the released sampler.  The transport distinction of
Section~\ref{sec:book-diffusion-transport} applies in this joint space under the model's time
and noise conventions.

Rosetta supervision fine-tunes atomic reconstruction: energy penalties accompany reconstruction
losses, the encoder stays fixed, and low-rank adapters modify the decoder (Supplementary
Section S4.3.1).  This does not identify a Rosetta Boltzmann law for the complete generator.
External search further changes the selected law through user metrics, branching, pruning, and
evaluation budget; best-of-noise training selection, interpolated conditioning fields, and score
rescaling are further instances of the same operation on the realized law
(Definition~\ref{def:selection-layer} and Proposition~\ref{prop:selection-composite}).  The
resulting predictor-based passing rate and an experimental binding or catalytic endpoint remain
separate measurements.

\subsection{Three tests suggested by the translation}

Compare additive and Hadamard writes under matched interventions, references, and readouts,
measuring both the centered write and the output response.  Then fix a noisy input and vary
recurrent depth, reporting homogeneous propagation separately from the forced response in
Eq.~\ref{eq:rfd4-forced-response}; full-generation comparisons must match compute.
Finally, carry selected source substitutions through sequence and coordinate readouts to
structural and mutational endpoints.  Two further protocols accompany the gauge and commitment
subsections: replicate audits over redrawn condition gauges at fixed invariant content, and
commitment curves measured under a declared rollout protocol alongside the search thresholds
that read them.  Replicate seeds or shared random numbers can control sampling variation, while
finite audits must retain changes of discrete branch.

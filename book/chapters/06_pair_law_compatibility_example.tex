\section*{Three valid pair laws with no joint law}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Three valid pair laws with no joint law}

Pairwise normalization does not guarantee global compatibility.  Let
$X_1,X_2,X_3\in\{0,1\}$ have uniform one-site marginals.  Consider three individually valid pair
laws:
\begin{equation}
X_1=X_2\quad\text{almost surely},
\qquad
X_2=X_3\quad\text{almost surely},
\qquad
X_1\ne X_3\quad\text{almost surely}.
\label{eq:book-incompatible-pair-laws}
\end{equation}
Each statement is realized by a normalized $2\times2$ table with the required marginals.  The first
two statements imply $X_1=X_3$ for every triple in the support, contradicting the third.  No joint
law on $(X_1,X_2,X_3)$ can therefore have all three pair marginals.

The example separates local and global probability claims.  Matrix scaling can turn each positive
pair score into a valid coupling.  A collection of those couplings belongs to one sequence law only
when their shared marginals and higher-order compatibility constraints can be satisfied
simultaneously.  A sparse layer of pair tables does not supply that global certificate by itself.
This does not prevent finite pair \emph{potentials} from being added into a globally normalized
Gibbs Hamiltonian.  It says that separately normalized pair \emph{marginals} need not be the
marginals of that Gibbs law, or of any common joint law.
